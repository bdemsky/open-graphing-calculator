%% CAS help: algebra commands
@group cas-algebra Algebra
Commands that rewrite expressions: simplifying, expanding, factoring, working with fractions and polynomials, and switching between exact and decimal values.

@cmd simplify
group: cas-algebra
summary: Simplify an expression
syntax: simplify(expr)
example: simplify(sin(x)^2+cos(x)^2)
result: 1
example: simplify((x^2-1)/(x-1))
result: x+1
example: simplify(exp(2*ln(x)))
result: x^2
see: normal, trigsimp, expand, factor

Tries several rewriting strategies (rational normal form, trig identities, logarithm and exponential rules, radical merging) and returns the simplest result found. If nothing simpler is found, the expression is returned as it is. For a predictable rewrite use a specific command such as [[normal]], [[expand]] or [[trigsimp]].

@cmd autosimplify
group: cas-algebra
summary: Quick simplification that keeps the form
syntax: autosimplify(expr)
example: autosimplify(cos(x)^2+sin(x)^2)
result: 1
example: autosimplify((x^2-1)/(x-1))
result: x+1
example: autosimplify(x*(x+1)-x^2)
result: x
example: autosimplify((x-1)*(x+1))
result: (x-1)*(x+1)
example: autosimplify((e^(i*x)+e^(-i*x))/2)
result: cos(x)
example: autosimplify(e^(i*x))
result: exp(i*x)
see: simplify, normal, trigsimp, exp2trig, mode-simplify

The quick pass that SIMPLIFY AUTO ([[mode-simplify]]) applies to every result on the home screen: the Pythagorean identities (sin² + cos² = 1, sec² − tan² = 1), the rational normal form when it is clearly simpler (like terms across products, common factors of a fraction, a sum of fractions over one denominator), Euler's formula when exponentials and trig functions of the same argument meet (`e^(i*x)` with `e^(-i*x)` or with sin/cos of x, `e^x` with `e^-x` or sinh/cosh of x: [[exp2trig]] or [[trig2exp]], taken only when the result is smaller, so `e^(i*x)-(cos(x)+i*sin(x))` is 0 and `(e^x+e^-x)/2` is cosh(x); not in DEGREE mode for sin/cos), and nothing else. A lone `e^(i*x)` or `cos(x)+i*sin(x)` stays as written. A factored or expanded polynomial, angle multiples such as `2*sin(x)*cos(x)`, decimals, units and uncertainties are kept as they are. Lists, matrices and equations are simplified element by element. [[simplify]] tries much more (factoring, trig and log rules, radicals).

@cmd forms
group: cas-algebra
summary: Alternate forms of an expression
syntax: forms(expr), forms(expr, 1)
example: forms(x^2-2*x+1)
result: [(x-1)^2,x^2-2*x+1]
example: forms((x+2)*(x-3))
result: [(x-3)*(x+2),x^2-x-6]
example: forms(1/(x-1)-1/(x+1))
result: [2/(x^2-1),2/((x-1)*(x+1)),1/(x-1)-1/(x+1)]
example: forms((x^2-1)/(x-1),1)
result: [[x+1,3,1,"x≠1"],[(x^2-1)/(x-1),11,0,""],[x^2/(x-1)-1/(x-1),17,0,""]]
example: forms(ln(x*y),1)
result: [[ln(x*y),6,1,""],[ln(x)+ln(y),9,0,"x>0, y>0"]]
see: simplify, factor, expand, partfrac, forms-browser

Lists different ways to write `expr`, the simplest first, like the "alternate forms" of a computer algebra website. It applies the rewriting commands ([[simplify]], [[normal]], [[factor]], [[expand]], [[partfrac]] and [[collect]] in each variable, [[texpand]], [[tlin]], [[trigsimp]], [[trig2exp]], [[exp2trig]], [[lnexpand]], [[lncollect]], [[expexpand]], rationalising a denominator) to `expr` and once more to each result, drops repeats, and checks every candidate numerically against `expr` at random real and complex points, so a wrong rewriting never shows up. The forms are sorted by size; those within 20 % of the smallest ("equally simple") come first. The input itself is always one of them, so an expression that is already as simple as it gets returns just itself.

`forms(expr, 1)` gives a row per form: [form, size, equally simple (1 or 0), condition]. The condition says where a form equals `expr` only on part of the domain: `"x≠1"` when a common factor was cancelled (the original is undefined there), `"x>0"` when it holds only for positive values (logarithm rules), `"x real"` when it fails for complex values. An empty condition means equal wherever both are defined.

The search has a time limit (about a second on the calculator; `forms(expr, d, n)` sets the work budget n, 800 by default) and a memory limit; when one runs out you get the forms found so far. On the home screen and in RPN the [[forms-browser|forms browser]] ({shift} {F4} {1}) shows the list in textbook form and lets you pick one.

@cmd expand
group: cas-algebra
summary: Multiply out products and powers
syntax: expand(expr)
example: expand((x+1)^3)
result: x^3+3*x^2+3*x+1
example: expand((a+b)*(a-b))
result: a^2-b^2
see: factor, collect, texpand, powexpand

Distributes products over sums and expands integer powers of sums, in any number of variables. Fractions are expanded term by term in the numerator. Use [[texpand]] to expand trig functions of sums.

@cmd factor
group: cas-algebra
summary: Factor a polynomial over the rationals
syntax: factor(expr)
example: factor(x^2-1)
result: (x-1)*(x+1)
example: factor(x^4+4)
result: (x^2-2*x+2)*(x^2+2*x+2)
example: factor(x^2*y-y^3)
result: y*(x+y)*(x-y)
see: expand, cfactor, sqrfree, factors, ifactor

Factors a polynomial (in any number of variables) into irreducible factors with rational coefficients. A rational function is factored in numerator and denominator. Use [[cfactor]] to split quadratic factors over the complex numbers and [[ifactor]] for integers.

@cmd cfactor
group: cas-algebra
summary: Factor over the complex numbers
syntax: cfactor(expr)
example: cfactor(x^2+1)
result: (x-i)*(x+i)
example: cfactor(x^4-1)
result: (x-1)*(x+1)*(x-i)*(x+i)
see: factor, csolve

Like [[factor]], but quadratic factors are further split into linear factors, which may have complex or irrational coefficients. Irreducible factors of degree 3 or more are kept.

@cmd sqrfree
group: cas-algebra
summary: Square-free factorization
syntax: sqrfree(expr)
example: sqrfree(x^3-x^2-x+1)
result: (x-1)^2*(x+1)
see: factor, factors

Writes a polynomial as a product of powers of square-free factors that are pairwise coprime. Cheaper than [[factor]]; the factors need not be irreducible.

@cmd factors
group: cas-algebra
summary: List of factors with multiplicities
syntax: factors(expr)
example: factors(x^3-x)
result: [[x,1],[x-1,1],[x+1,1]]
example: factors(x^2-2*x+1)
result: [[x-1,2]]
see: factor, roots

Returns the irreducible factors of a polynomial as a list of `[factor, multiplicity]` pairs. A constant content factor is not included.

@cmd collect
group: cas-algebra
summary: Group terms by powers of a variable
syntax: collect(expr)
syntax: collect(expr, x)
example: collect(a*x+b*x+c,x)
result: (a+b)*x+c
example: collect(x*y+x+y^2*x)
result: (y^2+y+1)*x
see: expand, coeffs

Rewrites a polynomial as a sum of powers of `x` whose coefficients are collected together. Without `x`, the main variable of the expression is used.

@cmd normal
group: cas-algebra
summary: Rational normal form
syntax: normal(expr)
example: normal(1/x+1/(x+1))
result: (2*x+1)/(x^2+x)
example: normal(x/(x^2-x))
result: 1/(x-1)
see: ratsimp, together, simplify, partfrac

Combines everything over a common denominator and cancels the greatest common divisor of numerator and denominator. [[ratsimp]] and [[together]] are the same command.

@cmd ratsimp
group: cas-algebra
summary: Same as normal
syntax: ratsimp(expr)
example: ratsimp((x^2-1)/(x+1))
result: x-1
see: normal, together

Same as [[normal]]: puts a rational expression over a common denominator and cancels common factors.

@cmd together
group: cas-algebra
summary: Combine fractions over a common denominator
syntax: together(expr)
example: together(1/a+1/b)
result: (a+b)/(a*b)
see: normal, partfrac

Same as [[normal]]: adds fractions over a common denominator and cancels common factors. The inverse operation is [[partfrac]].

@cmd partfrac
group: cas-algebra
summary: Partial fraction decomposition
syntax: partfrac(expr)
syntax: partfrac(expr, x)
example: partfrac(1/(x^2-1))
result: 1/(2*(x-1))-1/(2*(x+1))
example: partfrac((x^3+1)/(x^2+x-2))
result: x+2/(3*(x-1))+7/(3*(x+2))-1
see: apart, together, propfrac

## Refresher
P/Q with deg P < deg Q and Q factored: a linear factor (x − a)^m gives A₁/(x − a) + … + A_m/(x − a)^m, an irreducible quadratic (Bx + C)/(x² + px + q) per power. A₁ for a simple root: P(a)/Q'(a) (cover-up). Used for integrals and inverse Laplace transforms.
example: partfrac(1/(x^2*(x+1)))
result: 1/(x+1)-1/x+1/x^2
## Syntax
Splits a rational function of `x` into a polynomial part plus a sum of simple fractions, using factors over the rationals. Without `x`, the main variable is used. [[apart]] is the same command.

@cmd apart
group: cas-algebra
summary: Same as partfrac
syntax: apart(expr)
syntax: apart(expr, x)
example: apart((2*x+1)/(x^2+x))
result: 1/(x+1)+1/x
see: partfrac

Same as [[partfrac]]: partial fraction decomposition of a rational function.

@cmd subst
group: cas-algebra
summary: Substitute a value for a variable
syntax: subst(expr, x=a)
syntax: subst(expr, x, a)
syntax: subst(expr, [x=a, y=b])
example: subst(x^2+1,x=2)
result: 5
example: subst(x^2+y,x,3)
result: y+9
example: subst(x+y,[x=1,y=2])
result: 3
see: subs, horner, when

Replaces every occurrence of `x` in `expr` by `a` and simplifies the result. A list of equations substitutes several variables at once (simultaneously). [[subs]] is the same command.

@cmd subs
group: cas-algebra
summary: Same as subst
syntax: subs(expr, x=a)
syntax: subs(expr, [x=a, y=b])
example: subs(x+y,[x=1,y=2])
result: 3
see: subst

Same as [[subst]]: replaces variables by values.

@cmd numer
group: cas-algebra
summary: Numerator of a fraction
syntax: numer(expr)
example: numer(3/4)
result: 3
example: numer((x+1)/(x-1))
result: x+1
see: denom, normal

Returns the numerator of a number or of the rational form of an expression.

@cmd denom
group: cas-algebra
summary: Denominator of a fraction
syntax: denom(expr)
example: denom(3/4)
result: 4
example: denom((x+1)/(x-1))
result: x-1
see: numer, normal

Returns the denominator of a number or of the rational form of an expression.

@cmd quo
group: cas-algebra
summary: Quotient of polynomial division
syntax: quo(p, q)
syntax: quo(p, q, x)
example: quo(x^3+1,x+1)
result: x^2-x+1
example: quo(17,5)
result: 3
example: quo(-17,5)
result: -4
see: rem, divide, iquo, propfrac

Returns the quotient of the Euclidean division of polynomial `p` by `q` in the variable `x` (default: the main variable). For two integers it returns the floor of `p/q`, so `quo(-17,5)` is -4; see [[iquo]] for truncated division.

@cmd rem
group: cas-algebra
summary: Remainder of polynomial division
syntax: rem(p, q)
syntax: rem(p, q, x)
example: rem(x^3+2,x+1)
result: 1
example: rem(17,5)
result: 2
example: rem(-17,5)
result: 3
example: rem(17,-5)
result: -3
see: quo, divide, irem, mod

Returns the remainder of the Euclidean division of polynomial `p` by `q` in `x` (default: the main variable). Its degree is lower than the degree of `q`. For integers the remainder has the sign of `q`, like [[mod]].

@cmd divide
group: cas-algebra
summary: Quotient and remainder as a list
syntax: divide(p, q)
syntax: divide(p, q, x)
example: divide(x^3+2,x+1)
result: [x^2-x+1,1]
example: divide(17,5)
result: [3,2]
see: quo, rem

Returns `[quotient, remainder]` of the division of `p` by `q`, so that `p = quotient*q + remainder`.

@cmd gcd
group: cas-algebra
summary: Greatest common divisor
syntax: gcd(a, b, ...)
syntax: gcd(list)
example: gcd(12,18)
result: 6
example: gcd(x^2-1,x^2+2*x+1)
result: x+1
example: gcd(12,18,8)
result: 2
see: lcm, iegcd, content

Greatest common divisor of integers or polynomials (in any number of variables). Accepts two or more arguments, or a single list.

@cmd lcm
group: cas-algebra
summary: Least common multiple
syntax: lcm(a, b, ...)
syntax: lcm(list)
example: lcm(4,6)
result: 12
example: lcm(x^2-1,x+1)
result: x^2-1
see: gcd

Least common multiple of integers or polynomials. Accepts two or more arguments, or a single list.

@cmd degree
group: cas-algebra
summary: Degree of a polynomial
syntax: degree(p)
syntax: degree(p, x)
example: degree(x^3+2*x)
result: 3
example: degree(x^2*y^3,y)
result: 3
see: coeff, lcoeff, coeffs

Returns the degree of polynomial `p` in `x` (default: the main variable). Gives an error if `p` is not a polynomial in `x`.

@cmd coeff
group: cas-algebra
summary: Coefficient of a power of x
syntax: coeff(p, n)
syntax: coeff(p, x, n)
example: coeff(3*x^2+5*x+7,2)
result: 3
example: coeff(a*x^2+b*x*y,y,1)
result: b*x
see: coeffs, lcoeff, degree

Returns the coefficient of `x^n` in polynomial `p`. Without `x`, the main variable is used. Other variables are treated as constants.

@cmd coeffs
group: cas-algebra
summary: List of coefficients
syntax: coeffs(p)
syntax: coeffs(p, x)
example: coeffs(3*x^2+5*x+7)
result: [3,5,7]
example: coeffs(x^3-1)
result: [1,0,0,-1]
see: coeff, proot, lcoeff

Returns the list of all coefficients of `p` in `x`, from the highest power down to the constant term (including zeros).

@cmd lcoeff
group: cas-algebra
summary: Leading coefficient
syntax: lcoeff(p)
syntax: lcoeff(p, x)
example: lcoeff(3*x^2+5*x+7)
result: 3
example: lcoeff(a*x^2+b,x)
result: a
see: coeff, degree

Returns the coefficient of the highest power of `x` in `p`.

@cmd content
group: cas-algebra
summary: GCD of the coefficients
syntax: content(p)
syntax: content(p, x)
example: content(6*x^2+4*x)
result: 2
see: primpart, gcd

Returns the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of `p` in `x`. `p` equals `content(p)*primpart(p)`.

@cmd primpart
group: cas-algebra
summary: Primitive part of a polynomial
syntax: primpart(p)
syntax: primpart(p, x)
example: primpart(6*x^2+4*x)
result: 3*x^2+2*x
see: content

Divides `p` by its [[content]], leaving a polynomial whose coefficients have no common factor.

@cmd resultant
group: cas-algebra
summary: Resultant of two polynomials
syntax: resultant(p, q)
syntax: resultant(p, q, x)
example: resultant(x^2-2,x-1)
result: -1
example: resultant(x^2+y,x-y,x)
result: y^2+y
see: discriminant, gbasis

Computes the resultant of `p` and `q` with respect to `x`. It is zero exactly when `p` and `q` have a common root; with other variables present it eliminates `x`.

@cmd discriminant
group: cas-algebra
summary: Discriminant of a polynomial
syntax: discriminant(p)
syntax: discriminant(p, x)
example: discriminant(a*x^2+b*x+c,x)
result: b^2-4*a*c
example: discriminant(x^2-5*x+6)
result: 1
see: resultant, solve

Returns the discriminant of `p` in `x`. It is zero exactly when `p` has a repeated root.

@cmd horner
group: cas-algebra
summary: Evaluate a polynomial at a point
syntax: horner(p, a)
syntax: horner(p, a, x)
example: horner(x^3+2*x+1,2)
result: 13
see: subst

Evaluates the polynomial `p` at `x = a` (default variable: the main variable of `p`).

@cmd lagrange
group: cas-algebra
summary: Interpolating polynomial through points
syntax: lagrange(xlist, ylist)
syntax: lagrange(xlist, ylist, x)
example: lagrange([1,2,3],[1,4,9])
result: x^2
example: lagrange([0,1],[1,3],t)
result: 2*t+1
see: seq

Returns the polynomial of lowest degree that takes the values `ylist` at the points `xlist`, as an expanded polynomial in `x` (default `x`).

@cmd texpand
group: cas-algebra
summary: Expand trig functions of sums
syntax: texpand(expr)
example: texpand(sin(2*x))
result: 2*cos(x)*sin(x)
example: texpand(cos(x+y))
result: cos(x)*cos(y)-sin(x)*sin(y)
see: tlin, tcollect, expand

Uses addition and multiple-angle formulas to rewrite trig functions of sums and integer multiples in terms of trig functions of single variables.

@cmd tlin
group: cas-algebra
summary: Linearize products of trig functions
syntax: tlin(expr)
example: tlin(sin(x)^2)
result: 1/2-cos(2*x)/2
example: tlin(sin(x)*cos(x))
result: sin(2*x)/2
see: tcollect, texpand

Replaces products and powers of sines and cosines by sums of sines and cosines of multiple angles. Useful before integrating.

@cmd tcollect
group: cas-algebra
summary: Collect trig terms into multiple angles
syntax: tcollect(expr)
example: tcollect(sin(x)*cos(x))
result: sin(2*x)/2
see: tlin, texpand

Linearizes products of trig functions (like [[tlin]]) and then combines terms with the same angle.

@cmd trigsimp
group: cas-algebra
summary: Simplify trigonometric expressions
syntax: trigsimp(expr)
example: trigsimp(sin(x)^2+cos(x)^2)
result: 1
example: trigsimp(tan(x)*cos(x))
result: sin(x)
example: trigsimp(1-cos(x)^2)
result: sin(x)^2
see: simplify, tlin, texpand

Simplifies using trig identities such as `sin(x)^2+cos(x)^2=1` and `tan(x)=sin(x)/cos(x)`.

@cmd trig2exp
group: cas-algebra
summary: Rewrite trig functions as exponentials
syntax: trig2exp(expr)
example: trig2exp(cos(x))
result: exp(i*x)/2+exp(-i*x)/2
see: exp2trig

Rewrites sin, cos, tan and the other trig functions in terms of `exp(i*x)`.

@cmd exp2trig
group: cas-algebra
summary: Rewrite complex exponentials as trig
syntax: exp2trig(expr)
example: exp2trig(exp(i*x))
result: cos(x)+i*sin(x)
example: exp2trig(exp(i*x)+exp(-i*x))
result: 2*cos(x)
see: trig2exp

Rewrites exponentials with imaginary arguments using Euler's formula `exp(i*x)=cos(x)+i*sin(x)`.

@cmd lnexpand
group: cas-algebra
summary: Expand logarithms of products
syntax: lnexpand(expr)
example: lnexpand(ln(x^2*y))
result: 2*ln(x)+ln(y)
see: lncollect

Splits logarithms of products, quotients and powers into sums of logarithms: `ln(a*b)` becomes `ln(a)+ln(b)` and `ln(a^n)` becomes `n*ln(a)`. The rules assume positive arguments.

@cmd lncollect
group: cas-algebra
summary: Combine sums of logarithms
syntax: lncollect(expr)
example: lncollect(ln(x)+ln(y))
result: ln(x*y)
see: lnexpand

Combines sums and multiples of logarithms into a single logarithm, the inverse of [[lnexpand]].

@cmd expexpand
group: cas-algebra
summary: Expand exponentials
syntax: expexpand(expr)
example: expexpand((exp(x)+1)^2)
result: 2*exp(x)+exp(2*x)+1
example: expexpand(exp(x+y))
result: exp(x)*exp(y)
see: powexpand, expand

Expands like [[expand]] and then splits exponentials of sums, `exp(a+b) = exp(a)*exp(b)`. The split product is kept as it is; a later calculation with it merges the exponentials again.

@cmd powexpand
group: cas-algebra
summary: Expand powers
syntax: powexpand(expr)
example: powexpand(2^(x+1))
result: 2*2^x
example: powexpand((x*y)^3)
result: x^3*y^3
see: expand, expexpand

Same as [[expand]]: splits powers of products and powers with sums in the exponent, and multiplies out.

@cmd exact
group: cas-algebra
summary: Convert decimals to exact fractions
syntax: exact(expr)
example: exact(0.25)
result: 1/4
example: exact(1.5*x)
result: 3*x/2
see: approx, evalf

Replaces each decimal number in `expr` by a nearby fraction, turning an approximate result into an exact one.

@cmd approx
group: cas-algebra
summary: Numeric value of an expression
syntax: approx(expr)
syntax: approx(expr, n)
example: approx(pi)
result: 3.14159265359
example: approx(sqrt(2),30)
result: 1.41421356237309504880168872421
see: evalf, float, exact, mode

Evaluates `expr` numerically. With `n`, uses `n` significant digits (up to 5000, arbitrary precision for constants); otherwise the current display digits setting. Symbols without a value stay symbolic. [[evalf]] and [[float]] are the same command.

@cmd evalf
group: cas-algebra
summary: Same as approx
syntax: evalf(expr)
syntax: evalf(expr, n)
example: evalf(e)
result: 2.71828182846
example: evalf(pi,20)
result: 3.1415926535897932385
see: approx, float

Same as [[approx]]: numeric evaluation, optionally with `n` significant digits.

@cmd float
group: cas-algebra
summary: Same as approx
syntax: float(expr)
syntax: float(expr, n)
example: float(2/3)
result: 0.666666666667
see: approx, evalf

Same as [[approx]]: numeric evaluation, optionally with `n` significant digits.

@cmd propfrac
group: cas-algebra
summary: Proper fraction form
syntax: propfrac(a/b)
syntax: propfrac(p/q, x)
example: propfrac(17/5)
result: 2/5+3
example: propfrac((x^2+1)/(x-1))
result: x+2/(x-1)+1
see: partfrac, quo

Writes a fraction as an integer plus a proper fraction, or a rational function as a polynomial plus a fraction whose numerator has lower degree than the denominator.
