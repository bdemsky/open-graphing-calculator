%% CAS help: calculus commands
@group cas-calculus Calculus
Derivatives, integrals, limits, series, sums and products, vector calculus, Laplace transforms and differential equations.

@cmd fourier
group: cas-calculus
summary: Fourier transform
syntax: fourier(f, x, w)
example: fourier(exp(-x^2),x,w)
result: sqrt(2)*exp(-w^2/4)/2
example: fourier(exp(-abs(x)),x,w)
result: sqrt(2)/(sqrt(pi)*w^2+sqrt(pi))
example: fourier(1/(1+x^2),x,w)
result: sqrt(2)*exp(-abs(w))*sqrt(pi)/2
example: fourier(heaviside(x+1)-heaviside(x-1),x,w)
result: sqrt(2)*sin(w)/(sqrt(pi)*w)
example: fourier(cos(3*x),x,w)
result: sqrt(2)*dirac(w-3)*sqrt(pi)/2+sqrt(2)*dirac(w+3)*sqrt(pi)/2
example: fourier(exp(-a*abs(x)),x,w)
result: when(a>0,a*sqrt(2)/(sqrt(pi)*(a^2+w^2)),undef)
see: ifourier, laplace, ztrans

## Refresher
This calculator: F(ω) = (1/√(2π)) ∫ f(x) exp(−iωx) dx over ℝ (unitary, angular frequency); the inverse has exp(+iωx) and the same factor.
- f' → iωF; x f → iF'; f(x − a) → exp(−iaω) F; convolution f*g → √(2π) F G; Parseval ∫ |f|² dx = ∫ |F|² dω.
- Other conventions: without the factor (common in engineering) the transform is √(2π) F(ω); with exp(−2πikx) it is √(2π) F(2πk).
- Table: [[ref-transforms]].
example: fourier(exp(-x^2/2),x,w)
result: exp(-w^2/2)
## Syntax
The unitary transform with angular frequency: F(w) = 1/sqrt(2 pi) times the integral of f(x) exp(-i w x) dx over the real line; [[ifourier]] inverts it. Rules, applied with linearity:

- constants give `dirac(w)`, `dirac(x-a)` gives exp(-i a w)/sqrt(2 pi);
- shift and scaling: g(a x+b) gives exp(i w b/a) G(w/a)/|a| (for arguments of abs, sign, heaviside);
- modulation: exp(i k x), cos(k x), sin(k x) times g shift G by k; x^n g gives i^n times the n-th derivative of G;
- `heaviside(x)`, `sign(x)` as distributions; `heaviside(x)` times a decaying g by the Laplace transform at s = i w;
- Gaussians P(x) exp(-a x^2+b x+c) with a > 0;
- rational functions with poles off the real axis by residues (results with abs(w) or sign(w));
- functions decaying at both ends (exp(-a abs(x)) ...) as two one-sided Laplace transforms;
- a parameter of unknown sign that decides whether the transform exists: the result with its condition, `when(a>0, F, undef)` (after `assume(a>0)` just F).

Other conventions: SymPy's fourier_transform(f, x, k) (exponent -2 pi i x k) equals sqrt(2 pi) F(2 pi k). Needs the advanced CAS module.

@cmd ifourier
group: cas-calculus
summary: Inverse Fourier transform
syntax: ifourier(F, w, x)
example: ifourier(exp(-w^2/2),w,x)
result: exp(-x^2/2)
example: ifourier(sqrt(2/pi)/(1+w^2),w,x)
result: exp(-abs(x))
see: fourier

## Refresher
f(x) = (1/√(2π)) ∫ F(ω) exp(iωx) dω, so ifourier(fourier(f)) = f; at a jump the inverse integral gives the mean of the two sides. A wide Gaussian in ω is a narrow one in x (uncertainty: the widths multiply to at least ½).
example: ifourier(exp(-2*w^2),w,x)
result: exp(-x^2/8)/2
## Syntax
f(x) = 1/sqrt(2 pi) times the integral of F(w) exp(i w x) dw, the inverse of [[fourier]] (computed as fourier(F, w, -x)). Needs the advanced CAS module.

@cmd ztrans
group: cas-calculus
summary: z-transform of a sequence
syntax: ztrans(f, n, z)
example: ztrans(1,n,z)
result: z/(z-1)
example: ztrans(n*3^n,n,z)
result: 3*z/(z-3)^2
example: ztrans(sin(n),n,z)
result: sin(1)*z/(z^2-2*cos(1)*z+1)
see: iztrans, laplace, sum

## Refresher
X(z) = Σ x(n) z⁻ⁿ, n = 0, 1, 2, … (one-sided), for |z| larger than the largest pole.
- x(n + 1) → z X − z x(0); x(n − 1) → X/z (x = 0 before 0); aⁿ x(n) → X(z/a); n x(n) → −z X'(z); convolution → X Y.
- It does for recurrences what Laplace does for ODEs. Table: [[ref-transforms]].
example: ztrans(a^n,n,z)
result: z/(z-a)
## Syntax
The unilateral transform: the sum of f(n) z^(-n) over n = 0, 1, 2, ... Rules: linearity; a^n g(n) gives G(z/a); n^k g(n) gives (-z d/dz)^k G; cos, sin, cosh, sinh of a n+b; 1/n! gives exp(1/z); binomial(n,k) gives z/(z-1)^(k+1); anything else is summed with [[sum]]. Needs the advanced CAS module.

@cmd iztrans
group: cas-calculus
summary: Inverse z-transform
syntax: iztrans(F, z, n)
example: iztrans(z/(z-2),z,n)
result: 2^n
example: iztrans(z^2/((z-1)*(z-1/2)),z,n)
result: 2-(1/2)^n
example: iztrans(z/(z^2+1),z,n)
result: sin(n*pi/2)
example: iztrans(1/(z-1),z,n)
result: 1-piecewise(n=0,1,0)
see: ztrans

## Refresher
x(n) from X(z): partial fractions of X(z)/z, then z/(z − p) → pⁿ. All poles inside the unit circle ⇒ x(n) → 0 (a stable system).
example: iztrans(z/(z-1/2),z,n)
result: (1/2)^n
## Syntax
The sequence f(n), n >= 0, whose [[ztrans]] is the rational function F(z) (degree of the numerator at most that of the denominator). Partial fractions of F(z)/z with the poles found exactly: z/(z-p)^j gives binomial(n,j-1) p^(n-j+1); a pole at 0 gives the unit impulse `piecewise(n=k,1,0)`; a pair of complex poles r exp(+-i t) of a real F gives r^n times cos(n t) and sin(n t). Needs the advanced CAS module.

@cmd diff
group: cas-calculus
summary: Derivative of an expression
syntax: diff(expr, x)
syntax: diff(expr, x, n)
syntax: diff(expr, x, y, ...)
syntax: diff(expr, [x, y])
suffix: ,x)
example: diff(x^3,x)
result: 3*x^2
example: diff(x*sin(x),x)
result: cos(x)*x+sin(x)
example: diff(x^5,x,3)
result: 60*x^2
see: integrate, nderiv, grad, implicitdiff, tangent

## Refresher
f'(a) = lim (f(a+h) − f(a))/h as h → 0; the slope of the tangent, the best linear approximation f(a+h) ≈ f(a) + f'(a) h.
- Product, quotient and chain rules and a table: [[ref-calculus]].
- `diff(f,x,n)` is the n-th derivative; `diff(f,x,y)` the mixed partial ∂²f/∂x∂y (equal to ∂²f/∂y∂x for smooth f).
example: diff(exp(x)*cos(x),x)
result: cos(x)*exp(x)-exp(x)*sin(x)
## Syntax
Differentiates `expr` with respect to `x`. With an integer `n`, returns the n-th derivative. Several variables give a mixed partial derivative; a list of variables gives the list of partial derivatives (the gradient).

- `d/dx(expr)` is another way to write `diff(expr,x)`.
- For a function defined with `f(x):=...`, `f'(a)` and `f''(a)` give derivatives at a point.

@cmd integrate
group: cas-calculus
summary: Indefinite or definite integral
syntax: integrate(expr, x)
syntax: integrate(expr, x, a, b)
suffix: ,x)
example: integrate(x^2,x)
result: x^3/3
example: integrate(x*exp(x),x)
result: exp(x)*(x-1)
example: integrate(exp(-x),x,0,inf)
result: 1
example: integrate(1/sqrt(1-x^4),x)
result: elliptic_f(asin(x),-1)
example: integrate(x^2/sqrt(1-x^4),x)
result: elliptic_e(asin(x),-1)-elliptic_f(asin(x),-1)
example: integrate(1/sqrt(1+x^4),x)
result: elliptic_f(2*atan(x),1/2)/2
see: int, nint, diff, sum

## Refresher
∫ f dx = F + C with F' = f; ∫ f dx from a to b = F(b) − F(a) (fundamental theorem).
- The constant C is not shown. ∫ dx/x appears as ln(x); on x < 0 read ln|x|.
- A pole inside [a, b] makes the integral divergent (an error); an improper integral with inf converges or not.
- No closed form: returned unevaluated; use [[nint]] for a number.
- Techniques (parts, substitution, partial fractions) and a table: [[ref-calculus]].
example: integrate(x*exp(-x),x,0,inf)
result: 1
## Syntax
With two arguments, returns an antiderivative of `expr` with respect to `x` (no constant of integration is added). With limits `a` and `b`, returns the definite integral.

- Limits may be `inf` or `-inf` (improper integrals).
- If no closed form is found, the integral is returned unevaluated, for example `integrate(exp(x^3),x)`.
- A definite integral over an interval containing a pole gives an error (divergent). For a numeric value use [[nint]] or [[approx]].
- `integrate(expr, x, b)` integrates from 0 to `b`.
- Rational functions: denominators whose roots are found exactly (such as x^3+2) give ln and atan terms over the reals.
- With the advanced CAS module (standard and advanced tiers) more integrals have a closed form: radicals of one or two linear factors such as `(a+b*x)^(1/3)/(c+d*x)^(1/3)` or `sqrt(c-c/(a*x))`, exponentials of `atanh`, `atan`, `asinh`, `acosh`, powers of `asin(c*x)` and the other inverse functions times algebraic factors, denominators like `a+b*x^3` or `a+b*x^2+c*x^4` with symbolic coefficients, and results with `Ei`, `Si`, `Ci`, `erf` and `erfi` (`exp(a+b*x)/x^2`, `sin(b*x)/(c+d*x)`, `x^2*exp(-b*x^2)`, `x^3/ln(x)`). Each such result is checked by differentiation at sample points before it is returned.
- `piecewise(x<a, f, g)`, `heaviside`, `sign` and `abs` of a linear argument give a continuous antiderivative written with `heaviside`, e.g. `integrate(piecewise(x<0,-x,x),x)` is `heaviside(x)*x^2-x^2/2`.
- Elliptic integrals (advanced CAS module): a rational function times square roots whose product is a cubic or quartic polynomial, such as `1/sqrt(1-x^4)`, `sqrt(1+x^3)` or `sqrt(a+b*x^2)/sqrt(c+d*x^2)`, and the same with `cos`, `sin` or `cosh` in place of x (`1/sqrt(1+cos(x)^2)`, `sqrt(a+b*sec(x))`), give `elliptic_f`, `elliptic_e` and `elliptic_pi` in Legendre's form. Powers `sin(x)^p*cos(x)^q` with p or q not an integer give `hyp2f1`.

@cmd limit
group: cas-calculus
summary: Limit of an expression
syntax: limit(expr, x, a)
syntax: limit(expr, x, a, dir)
syntax: limit(expr, x=a)
suffix: ,x,0)
example: limit(sin(x)/x,x,0)
result: 1
example: limit((1+1/n)^n,n,inf)
result: e
example: limit(1/x,x,0,1)
result: inf
see: series, diff

## Refresher
lim f(x) = L as x → a: |f(x) − L| < ε whenever 0 < |x − a| < δ.
- Two-sided unless dir = 1 (right) or −1 (left); `undef` when the sides differ.
- 0/0 and ∞/∞: lim f/g = lim f'/g'. Standard: sin x/x → 1, (1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e, x ln x → 0 as x → 0⁺.
example: limit(x*ln(x),x,0,1)
result: 0
## Syntax
Returns the limit of `expr` as `x` tends to `a`.

- `a` may be `inf` or `-inf`.
- `dir` = 1 gives the limit from the right, -1 from the left; without it the limit is two-sided and `undef` is returned if the one-sided limits differ.
- Results can be `inf`, `-inf` or `undef`. An error is given if the limit could not be determined.
- At `inf`, factorials and gamma functions are handled by Stirling's formula, and a difference of exponentials in a numerator by taking one of them out (advanced CAS module): `limit(n!^(1/n)/n,n,inf)` is `exp(-1)`.

@cmd series
group: cas-calculus
summary: Series expansion around a point
syntax: series(expr, x)
syntax: series(expr, x, a, n)
syntax: series(expr, x=a, n)
suffix: ,x,0,5)
example: series(sin(x),x,0,5)
result: x-1/6*x^3+O(x^5)
example: series(exp(x),x,0,3)
result: 1+x+1/2*x^2+O(x^3)
example: series(1/x+x,x,0,3)
result: 1/x+x+O(x^3)
see: taylor, limit

## Refresher
f(x) = Σ f⁽ⁿ⁾(a) (x − a)ⁿ/n!; the order term O((x − a)ⁿ) says what is left out.
- The last argument n is the order: the terms of degree below n (not the number of nonzero terms).
- Poles give Laurent series (negative powers). Common series and convergence tests: [[ref-series]].
example: series(exp(x)*sin(x),x,0,5)
result: x+x^2+1/3*x^3+O(x^5)
## Syntax
Expands `expr` as a power series in `x` around `a` (default 0) and returns the terms of degree less than `n` (default 6). The result is shown in ascending powers of `x-a` (of `1/x` at `inf`) with the order term `O((x-a)^n)`; the value itself (`ans`, a variable it is stored in, a graph) is the polynomial without the order term.

- Laurent series (negative powers) and fractional powers are supported.
- [[taylor]] is the same command.

@cmd taylor
group: cas-calculus
summary: Same as series
syntax: taylor(expr, x, a, n)
syntax: taylor(expr, x=a, n)
suffix: ,x,0,5)
example: taylor(cos(x),x,0,4)
result: 1-1/2*x^2+O(x^4)
example: taylor(ln(x),x,1,3)
result: x-1-1/2*(x-1)^2+O((x-1)^3)
see: series

## Refresher
Taylor polynomial f(a) + f'(a)(x − a) + … + f⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾(a)(x − a)ⁿ⁻¹/(n − 1)!; the remainder is f⁽ⁿ⁾(c)(x − a)ⁿ/n! for some c between a and x. Series of common functions: [[ref-series]].
example: taylor(ln(x),x,1,4)
result: x-1-1/2*(x-1)^2+1/3*(x-1)^3+O((x-1)^4)
## Syntax
Same as [[series]]: Taylor polynomial of `expr` in `x` around `a` with the terms of degree less than `n` (defaults: `a`=0, `n`=6).

@cmd sum
group: cas-calculus
summary: Sum of a sequence of terms
syntax: sum(expr, k, a, b)
syntax: sum(list)
example: sum(k,k,1,n)
result: n*(n+1)/2
example: sum(1/k^2,k,1,inf)
result: pi^2/6
example: sum([1,2,3])
result: 6
example: sum(sin((2*k-1)*x),k,1,n)
result: sin(n*x)^2/sin(x)
example: sum(binomial(n,k)/(k+1),k,0,n)
result: (2^(n+1)-1)/(n+1)
example: sum(k*k!,k,0,n)
result: (n+1)!-1
example: sum(binomial(n,k)^2,k,0,n)
result: binomial(2*n,n)
example: sum(binomial(n,k)*binomial(m,n-k),k,0,n)
result: binomial(m+n,m)
see: product, seq

## Refresher
Σ_k f(k) from a to b. Closed forms to recognize:
    Σ k = n(n+1)/2   Σ k² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6
    Σ k³ = (n(n+1)/2)²
    Σ r^k, k=0..n = (r^(n+1) − 1)/(r − 1)
    Σ C(n,k) x^k = (1 + x)ⁿ   Σ 1/k² = π²/6
- Hypergeometric terms (t(k+1)/t(k) rational in k): Gosper's algorithm decides whether Σ t(k) up to n has a hypergeometric closed form; Zeilberger's algorithm finds a recurrence in n for Σ over k = 0..n (advanced CAS module).
- Convergence tests: [[ref-series]].
example: sum(k^2,k,1,n)
result: n*(n+1)*(2*n+1)/6
## Syntax
Adds the values of `expr` for `k` from `a` to `b`. The bounds may be symbolic or `inf`, in which case a closed form is looked for; if none is found the sum is returned unevaluated. With a list, returns the sum of its elements.

Closed forms: polynomials times powers r^k, binomial sums, rational terms (telescoping, zeta values), sums of sin and cos of a k + b; with the advanced CAS module also binomial(n,k) R(k) with R rational and lower bounds other than 0, and hypergeometric terms (products of powers, factorials and binomials): Gosper's algorithm for sums with a symbolic upper bound, and Zeilberger's algorithm for sums over k = 0..n, which finds a recurrence in n and solves it. A closed form for a symbolic bound found by the module is checked against the explicit sum for small bounds.

## Refresher
Π_k f(k) from a to b. Telescoping: Π (1 + 1/k) = n + 1; Π k = n!; linear factors give gamma: Π (k + q), k = 1..n = gamma(n + q + 1)/gamma(q + 1).
example: product(k/(k+1),k,1,n)
result: 1/(n+1)
## Syntax
Multiplies the values of `expr` for `k` from `a` to `b`. Symbolic bounds are supported for rational terms; with the advanced CAS module any rational term whose factors are linear, through the gamma function (p k + q gives p^n gamma(b + 1 + q/p)/gamma(a + q/p)). With a list, returns the product of its elements.

@cmd grad
group: cas-calculus
summary: Gradient vector
syntax: grad(expr, [x, y, ...])
syntax: grad(expr, vars, coords)
example: grad(x^2+y^2,[x,y])
result: [2*x,2*y]
example: grad(x*y*z,[x,y,z])
result: [y*z,x*z,x*y]
example: grad(r^2*cos(t),[r,t],polar)
result: [2*cos(t)*r,-r*sin(t)]
see: hessian, jacobian, diff, divergence, laplacian, potential

## Refresher
∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y, ∂f/∂z): the direction of fastest increase, |∇f| the rate; ∇f is normal to the level set f = c.
- Directional derivative D_u f = ∇f·u (|u| = 1); tangent plane of f = c at p: ∇f(p)·(x − p) = 0.
- ∇×∇f = 0: gradient fields are conservative; [[potential]] goes back from F to f.
- Spherical [r,t,p] here: t the angle from the z axis, p the azimuth.
example: grad(x^2+y^2+z^2,[x,y,z])
result: [2*x,2*y,2*z]
## Syntax
Returns the list of partial derivatives of `expr` with respect to each variable.

`coords` is `cartesian`, `polar` ([r,t]), `cylindrical` ([r,t,z]) or `spherical` ([r,t,p], t the angle from the z axis, p the azimuth); the result has the components along the unit vectors of that system. The coordinate forms of grad, divergence, curl and laplacian are in the advanced CAS module.

@cmd hessian
group: cas-calculus
summary: Matrix of second derivatives
syntax: hessian(expr, [x, y, ...])
example: hessian(x^3,[x])
result: [[6*x]]
example: hessian(x^2*y^3,[x,y])
result: [[2*y^3,6*x*y^2],[6*x*y^2,6*x^2*y]]
see: grad, jacobian

## Refresher
H_ij = ∂²f/∂x_i∂x_j, symmetric for smooth f; f(p + h) ≈ f(p) + ∇f·h + ½ h·H h.
- At a critical point (∇f = 0): H positive definite ⇒ minimum, negative definite ⇒ maximum, eigenvalues of both signs ⇒ saddle ([[eigenvals]]).
example: eigenvals(hessian(x^2+3*x*y+y^2,[x,y]))
result: [-1,5]

Eigenvalues −1 and 5: the origin is a saddle.
## Syntax
Returns the Hessian matrix of `expr`: entry (i,j) is the second partial derivative with respect to the i-th and j-th variables.

@cmd jacobian
group: cas-calculus
summary: Jacobian matrix
syntax: jacobian([f1, f2, ...], [x, y, ...])
example: jacobian([x*y,x+y],[x,y])
result: [[y,x],[1,1]]
see: grad, hessian

## Refresher
J_ij = ∂f_i/∂x_j; f(p + h) ≈ f(p) + J h. Change of variables: dx dy = |det J| du dv (polar r, spherical ρ² sin φ). det J ≠ 0 ⇒ a local inverse with Jacobian J⁻¹; chain rule J(f∘g) = J(f)·J(g).
example: simplify(det(jacobian([r*cos(t),r*sin(t)],[r,t])))
result: r
## Syntax
Returns the matrix of partial derivatives: row i holds the derivatives of the i-th function with respect to each variable.

@cmd divergence
group: cas-calculus
summary: Divergence of a vector field
syntax: divergence([f1, f2, ...], [x, y, ...])
syntax: divergence(F, vars, coords)
example: divergence([x,y,z],[x,y,z])
result: 3
example: divergence([x^2,x*y],[x,y])
result: 3*x
example: divergence([r^2,0,0],[r,t,p],spherical)
result: 4*r
see: curl, grad, flux

## Refresher
∇·F = ∂P/∂x + ∂Q/∂y + ∂R/∂z for F = (P, Q, R): the net outflow per unit volume (source density).
- Divergence theorem: the outward flux through a closed surface ∂V is ∫∫∫ ∇·F dV; in the plane ∮ F·n ds = ∫∫ ∇·F dA.
- ∇·(∇×A) = 0; on simple regions ∇·F = 0 ⇔ F = ∇×A ([[vpotential]]).
example: divergence([x,y,z],[x,y,z])
result: 3

So the flux of (x, y, z) out of the unit sphere is 3 · 4π/3 = 4π (see [[flux]]).
## Syntax
Returns the sum of the partial derivatives of each component with respect to the corresponding variable. Both lists must have the same length. With `coords` (polar, cylindrical, spherical; see [[grad]]) the components are along that system's unit vectors and the scale factors are applied.

## Refresher
∇×F = (R_y − Q_z, P_z − R_x, Q_x − P_y): twice the local angular velocity of the flow; a plane field gives the scalar Q_x − P_y.
- Stokes: ∮ F·dr around ∂S = ∫∫ (∇×F)·n dS, the boundary counter-clockwise seen from the side n points to (right-hand rule); Green's theorem is the plane case.

example: curl([-y,x,0],[x,y,z])
result: [0,0,2]
## Syntax
Returns the curl (rotation) of a vector field with three components in three variables. A plane field gives the scalar curl dF2/dx - dF1/dy. With `coords` (see [[grad]]) the components are along that system's unit vectors.

∇²f = ∇·∇f = f_xx + f_yy + f_zz. Harmonic: ∇²f = 0 (mean-value property; maximum on the boundary).

@cmd potential

## Refresher
f with ∇f = F. On a simply connected domain F has one ⇔ ∇×F = 0 (plane: Q_x = P_y). Then ∫ F·dr from A to B = f(B) − f(A): path independent, 0 around closed curves.

## Refresher
An ODE of order n has an n-parameter general solution (constants c1 … cn here). y' = f(x,y), y(x₀) = y₀ has exactly one local solution when f and ∂f/∂y are continuous near (x₀, y₀).
- Linear: y = c1 y₁ + … + cn yn + y_p, where y₁ … yn are independent (Wronskian ≠ 0, [[wronskian]]) and y_p is any particular solution.
- Types, methods and what is solved here: [[ref-ode]].
- Write y', y'' (not dy/dx); the unknown is y and the variable x unless you give others.
example: dsolve(y''+y'-2*y=exp(x))
result: c1*exp(-2*x)+c2*exp(x)+exp(x)*(3*x-1)/9

exp(x) solves the homogeneous equation (root 1), so the particular solution needs x exp(x): resonance.
## Syntax
Finds the general solution `y` of a differential equation in `x`, written with `y'`, `y''`, ... Free constants are named `c1`, `c2`, ... Default unknown is `y`, default variable `x`. The general solution of a linear equation is written as c1 times one solution plus c2 times another, and so on, plus a particular solution.

- Supported: first order separable, linear and Bernoulli equations; linear equations with constant coefficients of any order.
- Initial conditions such as `y(0)=1` and `y'(0)=0` go in a list together with the equation and fix the constants.
- Systems: a list of first-order equations `u'=...` and a list of unknowns, linear with constant coefficients (a forcing term in t is allowed). The solution is [[expm]](A t) times the constants, plus a particular solution; it is returned as `[u=..., v=...]`.
- With the advanced CAS module also Riccati equations y' = a(x) y^2 + b(x) y + c(x) (through a rational particular solution, or the linear second-order equation for u with y = -u'/(a u)) and Bessel's equation x^2 y'' + x y' + (b x^2 - nu^2) y = 0 (`besselj`, `bessely`, or `besseli`, `besselk`).
- `dsolve(eq, y, x, x0, n)` gives a power series solution of a linear equation about x = x0 with the terms below (x-x0)^n: at an ordinary point every coefficient follows from the first ones (`c1` = y(x0), `c2` = y'(x0), ...); at a regular singular point of a second-order equation Frobenius' method gives (x-x0)^r times a series, with a logarithm in the second solution when the roots of the indicial equation differ by an integer. Conditions at x0 can be added as above.
- For a numeric solution use [[odesolve]].

@cmd pdesolve
group: cas-calculus
summary: Solve a partial differential equation
syntax: pdesolve(eq, u(x,t))

example: pdesolve(u_t(x,t)=u_xx(x,t),u(x,t),[u(0,t)=0,u(pi,t)=0,u(x,0)=sin(x)+3*sin(2*x)])
result: exp(-t)*sin(x)+3*exp(-4*t)*sin(2*x)
example: pdesolve(u_t(x,t)=u_xx(x,t),u(x,t),[u(0,t)=0,u(1,t)=0,u(x,0)=1],3)
result: 4*exp(-pi^2*t)*sin(pi*x)/pi+4*exp(-9*pi^2*t)*sin(3*pi*x)/(3*pi)
example: pdesolve(u_tt(x,t)=c^2*u_xx(x,t),u(x,t),[u(x,0)=exp(-x^2),u_t(x,0)=0])
result: exp(-(x+c*t)^2)/2+exp(-(x-c*t)^2)/2
example: pdesolve(u_xx(x,y)+u_yy(x,y)=0,u(x,y),[u(0,y)=0,u(1,y)=0,u(x,0)=0,u(x,1)=sin(pi*x)])
result: sin(pi*x)*sinh(pi*y)/sinh(pi)
see: pdenum, dsolve, laplacian

Solves a linear PDE for the unknown function `u(x,t)`. Write partial derivatives as `u_x(x,t)`, `u_xx(x,t)`, `u_t(x,t)`, `u_tt(x,t)` (or `diff(u(x,t),x)`, `diff(u(x,t),x,2)`). Conditions give u or a first derivative on a line: `u(0,t)=0`, `u_x(L,t)=0`, `u(x,0)=f(x)`, `u_t(x,0)=g(x)`.

- First order, a u_x + b u_t + c u = f: by characteristics. Without a condition the answer has an arbitrary function F; a condition such as `u(x,0)=g(x)` fixes it.
- Heat equation u_t = k u_xx and wave equation u_tt = c^2 u_xx on an interval: separation of variables, with u or u_x given at each end (constant values). The answer is a Fourier series: finite when the initial data is a sum of the modes, otherwise a sum to infinity; with a last argument `n` the first n terms.
- Laplace's equation u_xx + u_yy = 0 on a rectangle with u given on the four sides.
- On the whole line (only initial conditions): d'Alembert's formula for the wave equation, the heat kernel for the heat equation.
- Anything else, or an integral that cannot be done exactly, stays unevaluated; use [[pdenum]] for a numeric solution.

@cmd pdenum
group: cas-calculus
summary: Numeric solution of a PDE on a grid
syntax: pdenum(eq, u(x,t), [conditions], [x,a,b,nx], [t,t0,t1,nt] [, method])
example: dim(pdenum(u_t(x,t)=u_xx(x,t),u(x,t),[u(0,t)=0,u(1,t)=0,u(x,0)=sin(pi*x)],[x,0,1,10],[t,0,0.1,50]))
result: [51,11]
see: gridval, pdesolve, odesolve

Finite differences on nx steps in x and nt steps in t. Returns the matrix of values: row j is the time t0 + j (t1-t0)/nt, column i the point a + i (b-a)/nx.

- Heat equation u_t = k u_xx: explicit scheme (stable when k dt/dx^2 <= 1/2, an error otherwise) or Crank-Nicolson (method `cn`, always stable); without a method the explicit scheme is used when it is stable.
- Wave equation u_tt = c^2 u_xx: leapfrog scheme, stable when c dt/dx <= 1.
- Conditions: u or u_x at x = a and x = b (any expression in t), u(x,t0) and for the wave equation u_t(x,t0).
- Laplace and Poisson equation u_xx + u_yy = rho(x,y) on [a,b] x [c,d] (give [y,c,d,ny] instead of the time range) with u on the four sides: successive over-relaxation.
- At most 250000 grid points; the memory needed is checked first.

To see the result, store it (`U:=pdenum(...)`) and graph [[gridval]](U, [a,b], [t0,t1], x, y) in the 3D graph, or `gridval(U,[a,b],[t0,t1],x,t)` on a function graph and play the slider t with the animation.

example: minimize(x+y,[x>=1,y>=2],[x,y])
result: [3,[x=1,y=2]]
example: minimize(x+y,[x^2+y^2=1],[x,y])
result: [-sqrt(2),[x=-sqrt(2)/2,y=-sqrt(2)/2]]
see: maximize, linprog, solve, diff

## Refresher
Necessary conditions: f'(x) = 0 (one variable), ∇f = 0 (several); with constraints g = 0: ∇f = λ ∇g (Lagrange); with g ≤ 0: KKT (multipliers μ ≥ 0, μ g = 0). Sufficient: second derivative or Hessian positive (definite); for a convex problem any local minimum is global.
- Here: over all reals the infimum (maybe −inf); linear objective and constraints: a linear program ([[linprog]]); equality constraints: Lagrange, the least critical value (boundedness not checked). More: [[ref-optim]].
example: minimize(x^2+y^2,[x+2*y=5],[x,y])
result: [5,[x=1,y=2]]
## Syntax
The least value (the infimum) of `expr` over all real `x` where it is real, from the values at the real critical points (where diff(expr,x) = 0), the limits at -inf and inf and the one-sided limits at the ends of the domain set by `ln` and roots (x*ln(x) needs x > 0). Unevaluated when the critical points cannot be found. Needs the advanced CAS module.

With a list of constraints and a list of variables the result is `[value, [x=..., ...]]`:

- a linear `expr` with linear constraints (<=, >=, =; < and > count as <= and >=) is a linear program, solved exactly as in [[linprog]]; a variable can be negative unless a constraint `x>=0` says otherwise; an unbounded problem gives `[-inf,[]]` and an empty feasible set `[inf,[]]` (the least value of an empty set; for maximize the signs swap);
- otherwise the constraints must be equations (or the list empty): Lagrange multipliers, the real solutions of grad(expr) = l1 grad(g1) + ... with all g = 0 found by [[solve]], and the least value of `expr` among them (a critical value: the problem is not checked to be bounded).

## Refresher
max f = −min(−f): the same conditions with the inequalities reversed (Hessian negative definite at a maximum). See [[minimize]] and [[ref-optim]].
example: maximize(x*exp(-x),x)
result: exp(-1)
## Syntax
The greatest value (the supremum) of `expr` over all real `x`; see [[minimize]], also for `maximize(expr, constraints, vars)`. Needs the advanced CAS module.

The del operator in textbook notation. {CAS} has the four forms as templates (∇f, ∇·F, ∇×F, ∇²f); the CATALOG entry inserts ∇ alone.

- `∇f` is [[grad]] `grad(f)`, `∇·F` is [[divergence]], `∇×F` is [[curl]] and `∇²f` is [[laplacian]]. `∇^2 f` and `∇.F` work too, and the word `nabla` can stand for ∇ (`nabla.F`, `nabla^2 f`).
- ∇ applies to the next factor, like `sin x`: `∇f*g` is (∇f)*g and `∇x^2` is ∇(x^2). Put sums in parentheses: `∇(x^2+y^2)`.
- The variables are x, y, z: a vector field with 2 or 3 components uses [x,y] or [x,y,z]; for a scalar, [x,y,z] if z occurs, else [x,y] if y occurs, else [x]. So `∇(x*y)` is [y,x] but `∇(x*z)` is [z,0,x].
- Other variables: `∇_[r,t]f`, `∇_[r,t]·F` (or `∇·_[r,t]F`); after the `]` only · × ² work, not `.`. For polar, cylindrical or spherical coordinates put everything in parentheses, `∇(f,[r,t],polar)`, or use the commands.
- · and × multiply everywhere else (`2×3`); only right after ∇ do they mean divergence and curl.
- In a function definition the variables are found when it is called: after `g(x,y):=∇(x*y)`, `g(1,2)` differentiates the number 2. Name them there: `grad(x*y,[x,y])`.

∇² needs the advanced CAS module, like [[laplacian]].
