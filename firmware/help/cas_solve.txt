%% CAS help: equations and solving
@group cas-solve Equations and solving
Exact and numeric solutions of equations, inequalities and systems, and roots of polynomials.

@cmd solve
group: cas-solve
summary: Solve equations exactly
syntax: solve(eq, x)
syntax: solve([eq1, eq2], [x, y])
syntax: solve(ineq, x)
syntax: solve(eq, x, a, b)
suffix: ,x)
example: solve(x^2-4=0,x)
result: [-2,2]
example: solve(sin(x)=1/2,x)
result: [2*n_1*pi+pi/6,2*n_1*pi+5*pi/6]
example: solve(cos(x)=0,x)
result: [n_1*pi+pi/2]
example: solve(sin(x)=1/2,x,0,10)
result: [pi/6,5*pi/6,13*pi/6,17*pi/6]
example: solve(x^2<4,x)
result: [-2<x and x<2]
example: solve([x+y=3,x-y=1],[x,y])
result: [[x=2,y=1]]
see: csolve, fsolve, linsolve, roots

## Refresher
Real solutions of f(x) = 0 (or of a system). Polynomials: formulas up to degree 4 (exact here up to 3 and special forms); from degree 5 there is no general formula, so decimals.
- Periodic equations give every solution: n_1 stands for any integer (tan x = 1: π/4 + nπ).
- [[csolve]] for complex solutions, [[fsolve]] for a numeric root near a guess, `solve(eq,x,a,b)` for the solutions in [a, b].
- A system returns a list of solutions, each a list `[x=…, y=…]`; `[]` means no real solution was found.
example: solve(tan(x)=1,x)
result: [n_1*pi+pi/4]
## Syntax
Returns the list of real solutions of an equation for `x`. An expression without `=` is treated as `expr=0`. Without `x`, the main variable is used. An empty list means no real solution was found.

- Polynomials: exact up to degree 3 and for special forms such as `x^4=2`, otherwise decimal approximations.
- Trig equations give all solutions as families such as `pi/6+2*pi*n_1`, where `n_1` stands for any integer (it is marked as an integer, as with [[assume]]; if the name is taken, `n_2` and so on are used). Families that overlap are merged: `cos(x)=0` gives `pi/2+pi*n_1`. Substituting a family into the equation simplifies, for example `subst(sin(x),x,2*n_1*pi+pi/6)` gives `1/2`.
- `solve(eq, x, a, b)` lists the solutions between `a` and `b` (inclusive) as numbers in increasing order, at most 100 of them; `a` and `b` may be `-inf` and `inf` when there are finitely many solutions.
- Without the advanced CAS module, trig equations give the principal solutions in one period and the interval form is not available.
- Inequalities give conditions such as `-2<x and x<2`.
- Systems: give lists of equations and unknowns; each solution is a list of `var=value`.
- For complex solutions use [[csolve]]; for a numeric root of any equation use [[fsolve]].

@cmd csolve
group: cas-solve
summary: Solve equations over the complex numbers
syntax: csolve(eq, x)
syntax: csolve([eq1, eq2], [x, y])
suffix: ,x)
example: csolve(x^2+1=0,x)
result: [i,-i]
example: csolve(x^3=1,x)
result: [1,-sqrt(3)*i/2-1/2,sqrt(3)*i/2-1/2]
see: solve, cfactor, proot

Like [[solve]], but also returns non-real complex solutions.

@cmd fsolve
group: cas-solve
summary: Numeric solution of an equation
syntax: fsolve(eq, x)
syntax: fsolve(eq, x, guess)
syntax: fsolve(eq, x=guess)
syntax: fsolve([eqs], [vars], [guesses])
example: fsolve(cos(x)=x,x)
result: [0.739085133215]
example: fsolve(x^3-2*x-5,x,2)
result: 2.09455148154
example: fsolve([x^2+y^2=4,x=y],[x,y],[1,1])
result: [x=1.41421356237,y=1.41421356237]
see: nsolve, solve, proot

## Refresher
Newton's method x ← x − f(x)/f'(x) from the guess (quadratic convergence near a simple root; it may jump to another root or fail at f' = 0). Systems: x ← x − J⁻¹ F(x) with the [[jacobian]].
example: fsolve(cos(x)=x,x)
result: [0.739085133215]
## Syntax
Finds decimal solutions of an equation. Without a starting value, real roots are searched for and returned as a list; with a starting value `guess`, Newton's method returns the root closest to it. For systems (up to 10 unknowns) give a list of starting values. [[nsolve]] is the same command.

@cmd nsolve
group: cas-solve
summary: Same as fsolve
syntax: nsolve(eq, x)
syntax: nsolve(eq, x, guess)
example: nsolve(exp(x)=3,x)
result: [1.09861228867]
see: fsolve

Same as [[fsolve]]: numeric solution of an equation or system.

@cmd linsolve
group: cas-solve
summary: Solve a system of linear equations
syntax: linsolve([eq1, eq2, ...], [x, y, ...])
example: linsolve([x+y=3,x-y=1],[x,y])
result: [x=2,y=1]
example: linsolve([2*x+y=1,x+2*y=2],[x,y])
result: [x=0,y=1]
see: solve, rref, inv

Solves a linear system exactly by Gauss-Jordan elimination and returns the list `[x=..., y=...]`. Coefficients may be symbolic. Gives an error if the equations are not linear.

@cmd roots
group: cas-solve
summary: Roots of a polynomial with multiplicities
syntax: roots(p)
syntax: roots(p, x)
example: roots(x^2-3*x+2)
result: [[1,1],[2,1]]
example: roots(x^3-x^2)
result: [[1,1],[0,2]]
see: proot, solve, factors

Returns the roots of polynomial `p` in `x` as a list of `[root, multiplicity]` pairs.

@cmd proot
group: cas-solve
summary: Numeric roots of a polynomial
syntax: proot(p)
syntax: proot(coefflist)
example: proot(x^2-2)
result: [-1.41421356237,1.41421356237]
example: proot([1,0,-2])
result: [-1.41421356237,1.41421356237]
example: proot(x^3-1)
result: [1.0,-0.5-0.866025403784*i,0.866025403784*i-0.5]
see: roots, coeffs, fsolve

Returns decimal approximations of all complex roots of a polynomial, given as an expression or as a list of coefficients from the highest power down.

@cmd gbasis
group: cas-solve
summary: Groebner basis of polynomials
syntax: gbasis([p1, p2, ...], [x, y, ...])
syntax: gbasis(polys, vars, order)
example: gbasis([x^2+y,x*y-1],[x,y])
result: [y^3+1,x+y^2]
example: gbasis([x+y-1,x-y],[x,y])
result: [y-1/2,x-1/2]
see: solve, resultant

Returns the reduced Groebner basis of the ideal generated by the polynomials. The default monomial order is lexicographic (`plex`); give `tdeg` for graded reverse lexicographic order. With lex order the basis is triangular, which helps to solve polynomial systems.
