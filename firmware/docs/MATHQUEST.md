# Math Quest: math games for grades 3, 4 and 5

Math Quest is a code module (`modules/mathquest/`, interface `modx` ABI 1, see
[QSPI_CONTENT.md](QSPI_CONTENT.md) section 12) with short, playful math adventures for
children in US grades 3 and 4, and a grade ahead. It covers every Common Core standard of
grades 3, 4 and 5 and the grade-2 foundations, drills the multiplication and division facts
to fluency with spaced repetition, tells mistakes as moments of a story, and shows the
guides' tricks. Every answer is a game action: there is no question screen in normal play. It is kept **out of the release**: `make mathquest-module` builds
`MATHQnnn.MOD`, which is installed from the microSD card (Memory > Modules);
`make release` does not include it, and `MOD_MATHQUEST` (which would put it into the
factory content) is off in every tier.

## The game

**Worlds** (each a chapter with a goal and a guide from the cast; every world is open from the
start -- see "Exploration is free" below):

| World | Guide | Its games | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garden | Pip, a sprout | Bubble Pop, Build It (flower beds), Peekaboo, Cloud Catch | multiplication and division, stories, properties, patterns, factors |
| Post Office | Dot, a mail penguin | Gate Dash, Treasure Heist, Kart Race, Hop Along, Sort Slide | place value, comparing, rounding, +/-, multi-digit x and / |
| Bakery | Momo, an axolotl baker | Pie Shop, Hop Along, Sky Fling, Peekaboo, Sort Slide | fractions, equivalence, comparing, +/-, mixed numbers, decimals |
| Clock Tower | Olli, an owl | Clock Tower, Kart Race, Sort Slide | time, elapsed time, mass and volume, units |
| Shop | Tango, a fox kit | Shop Till, Bubble Pop | money, change, money as decimals |
| Art Studio | Dabble, a chick | Build It (rugs, fences), Sky Fling (angles), Sort Slide | area, perimeter, shapes, angles, lines, symmetry |
| Fair | Bramble, a hedgehog | Bubble Pop, Cloud Catch, Gate Dash | graphs, line plots, tables |
| Sky Castle | Nova, a star sprite | Treasure Heist, Kart Race, Gate Dash, Treasure Dig | grade 5: powers of ten, decimals, two-digit division, brackets, expressions, patterns, the coordinate plane |
| Star Harbor | Nova | Bubble Pop, Peekaboo, Sort Slide, Kart Race | grade 5: unlike fractions, fractions as division, fraction times fraction, scaling, unit fractions, units, fraction plots, volume, shape families |

Every world has 4 to 14 stops (each a place to play its game, 10 orders; Bubble Pop's rounds are 8)
and a **finale** (12 mixed orders) that gives a hat and introduces the next guide. Nothing waits for a
finale: every world, stop and finale is open from the start, and the stops' skills only say which
game the stop plays and which stories the place prefers. The orders themselves come from the engine
(`mq_engine.c`), wherever she plays; grade-5 orders come once she is ready (`mq_grade5_ready`, or
placed there), in any world.

**Entering a world** is not a level select (the old row of identical stops, the Bakery's fifteen pies,
read as level markers once everything was open): the screen asks "What shall we play?" and shows the
place's **2 to 4 different games**, each with its own picture and name, and the finale
(`mq_world_games`: the place's own games first -- the Bakery's Pie Shop, the Clock Tower's clocks, the
Shop's till and Pet Shop -- scored by how much of each is played here against everywhere). A pick goes
straight into a round of that game, the engine choosing every order; a world with nothing to choose
starts playing at once. Progress there shows as **the place getting fancier** with her stars: bunting,
then flowers along the ground, then friends who come to visit and dance, sparkles at full stars. The
stops remain inside (they say which game a pick plays, and their intros and tricks are still there).
`test_engine` checks every world: no stop buttons, 2 to 4 distinct choices, each pick plays its game.

**A card is a promise.** The playtest: in the Shop, "Shop Till" and "Pet Shop" started Bubble Pop -- the
round was the till's, but the engine picked maths the till cannot play (a sum, a clock), and such an
order went to the skill's own game. Now a round she picked (`mq_game_t.chosen`) is played in that game
from the first order to the last: the engine, the placement, a review that is due and the error
patterns' practice only take skills the game plays (`mq_game_plays`: one of the skill's games, the
friends' games included; a pick game such as Bubble Pop tells any skill a pick game tells), an order it
cannot show is made again, and a skill it could not make one of is set aside for the round
(`mq_round_plays`); a review or placement order it cannot play waits for another round.
`tests/unit/test_mq_cards.c` taps every card of every world for players of each grade (twice: new, then
with reviews and patterns about) and checks every order of the round; `tests/ui/49_mathquest_cards.txt` is
the playtest's path; the bot's `card-lands` (a world or arcade card lands on the game it names: state
`mq_cards` lists the screen's cards, `mq_here` where she is) and `round-game` (a picked round stays in its
game) run in every strategy, and its `cards` strategy taps every card of every world and the arcade.

**The Shop (a place) and the Store (the decorating store) are different things.** The Shop is a world
of the town with the money games (Shop Till, Pet Shop); the Store, reached from her room ({F1} "store"),
is where her coins buy things for her rooms, its title line "Store" (the Sparkle Store in the game's
words). They share the counter's conventions -- the same coins and bills, the picky cashier, the change
typed on the till's keys -- but they stay two experiences: a round of Shop Till is play (the engine's
orders, coins earned), the Store's checkout is real spending of what she earned (her own basket, her own
purse). Both count as money practice for the engine.

**A round is one game.** Each order (a problem) arrives on the order board **in full**, so it always
says what to find and in what: "Share 5.4 kg of berries equally into 6 baskets. How many kg go in each
basket?" (`mq_board_text`; the playtest: the old eight-word board, "5.4 kg into 6 baskets!", cut the
actual question). It is said aloud the same way. The scene's tag ("Bracelets for friends!") is an
extra in front when it costs no line, never a replacement; an order of one line keeps its stage
below it, a longer one (two short sentences, three lines at most) fills the board. Otherwise the story's things stand on a
stage with their numbers as in-world labels -- three bracelets with a "4" tag each and a bead jar
marked "?", a heap of 24 cookies and four plates marked "?", a clock at 8:15 + a 55 min timer ->
a clock marked "?" (`mq_stage.c`). Nothing on the stage can be counted for the answer (groups are
drawn only when their number is given, a heap carries one picture as its sign); after the answer
the scene fills in (the beads on the bracelets, the answer on the tag). A tap on the board tells
the whole story (it also comes with the why). Orders without a story have a short caption with
their numbers (`CAP` in `mq_gen.c`); `test_board_words` checks every skill in each of its games and
difficulties: the board fits, and it ends in a question that names what to find ("How many beads are
left?", not "How many are left?" or "The other?"), an instruction ("Set the clock for Olli, please!"),
or a sum with its unknown marked ("42 ÷ 7 = ?").
Every game is played in its world's place (`mq_scenery.c`): sky, far hills, near flowers and
animated details at two speeds (clouds, swaying flowers, steam from cups, turning gears, a ferris
wheel, waves and a lighthouse). The answer is what she does in the game. The 16 games (`mq_play.c`):

| Game | What she does | Controls |

Every skill has one to four games (`mq_skill_mech[]`, the favourite first), each round takes the
next of them, and an order from an earlier skill (the interleaved review) is **retold in the
round's game** when it fits, else in one of its own. Some games ask the same thing the other way
round, and the skill and its bookkeeping stay the same: "set the clock to 3:40" for reading a
clock, "a rug of 24 squares, 4 tall" for the area of a rug, "aim at 65 degrees" for reading a
protractor, "dig at (3, 5)" for naming a point, "find the friend whose trays make 24" for a
fact; the misconceptions are mapped for the retold question too (a rug whose perimeter is the
area: area and perimeter mixed up; the protractor's other scale; x and y swapped).

**A visit is long enough to settle in** (the user: "Some times it seems that i get pushed around shops too
quickly...maybe a little longer in each?"): a round is `MQ_RUN_N` = 10 orders (was 6), a finale `MQ_RUN_BOSS` = 12 (was 8),
Bubble Pop `MQ_RUN_POP` = 8 (was 5); the placement keeps its 8. A miss near the end still brings up to two easier extra
orders (it ends on a win). A long round marks its half in the story, not with a new scene: the cheer of the order that
reaches it says "Halfway! Yay!" and the order tickets in the top line shine (they sit closer together in a long round,
clear of the race's place). The facts' boxes count rounds, so a long round ticks their clock at the half too (their
spacing in orders stays as it was). Coins per round do not grow: an answer's coins are scaled by the old length over
the new (`pay_base / run_n`, the remainder carried, rounded); the per-round rewards (first finish, three stars,
finale) are unchanged, and three stars now mean 90 % right first time (all of a short round, one slip in ten).
**The done screen** offers, as widgets (`mq_widget.c`: a tap with the pressed look, the arrows and ENTER, a finger-sized
hit area): home, the kitchen (or the arcade), **"Stay & play again!"** -- another round of the same game in the same
place (a finale again after a finale), no fun beat in between, its coins and history kept like any round's (the break
and the "everything done" party still come first) -- and "next stop!". The focus starts on "next stop!", so ENTER, a
swipe or a tap anywhere else goes on as before; for 0.8 s after the round's last answer no tap or swipe is taken (her
taps in the game must not wave the screen away). `mq stay` is the console's way; `test_long_rounds` covers it all.

**Moments of a round** (`q->sub`): *play*; *cheer* (the friend's happy ending, a hug and a dance,
"+1" stars, the combo); *why* (after a second miss: the right answer in the game with its
picture); *offer* (two misses in a row: the guide offers the trick); *trick* (the worked example
as a little show in the round). Everything waits for her: there is no timer on thinking (speed
lives in the arcade). Pure fun streams between orders (stars, coins, plain bubbles, friends to
bop) and power-ups come with combos: the snail shell slows the field, the wand makes a dud fade
away (F2 or a tap on it). The **finale** has a grumble, a grumpy cloud whose mood meter fills
with every order until it smiles and dances.

**Fact Arcade** (always open): Star Catch, Cafe Rush, Blooming Garden, Snail Race (typed
answers: a speed round is a natural place for them), and the keypad action games below; the
fact games speed up as she goes and keep her fastest finish ("New best!"). The **toy box**
(below) is in the arcade too.

**Rewards**: stars for every order (more on a combo), a sticker for the first finish or a
three-star finish of a stop, hats from finales, coins, the round's stars against the game's best
("New best!"), and the unlock ladder (below).

**Game feel**: integer squash-and-stretch animations of the cast (idle, happy, think, dance,
pout, fly), sparkles, number pop-ups, a combo badge, a screen shake on big moments, an iris
between scenes opening from where she tapped. No sound on the calculator: all feedback is
visual (the sound hooks below feed the web page).

**Input**: touch first, with keys for everything (above); CLEAR goes back.

## Exploration is free; learning adapts to wherever she plays

A design principle since the playtest (being locked out annoyed her): **nothing is ever locked**.
Every world and building, every stop and finale, every game (Cake Kitchen and Pet Shop too), the
arcade and its action games, the toy box, every room, the merge board and the town are open from
the first minute; there are no padlocks, no "finish X to open Y", no scaffolding that blocks a door
and no locked tiles (`mq_world_open`, `mq_skill_unlocked`, `mq_room_open`, `mq_action_open`,
`mq_item_unlocked` all say yes; a test renders every kid screen and fails on a gate word). Goals --
friends' requests, story chapters, daily quests, collections, the finales -- are invitations that
point at where fun is happening and pay rewards; nothing is withheld for skipping them. Coins,
items, squishies and decorations are still earned by playing (the shop has things she cannot afford
yet, never things she may not have). "Finishing" is the grown-ups' view (every standard and fact),
never a gate.

**The engine chooses every order** (`mq_engine.c`), whatever she picks:

- **The placement** (the playtest: "it is jumping into some pretty hard problems"; the audit's D1): a
  new player's first games ask twelve ordinary orders that probe four strands in turn --
  multiplication and division, place value and sums, fractions, measurement -- starting at
  **mid-grade 3** (difficulty 1), a grade up only after **two rights in a row** at a grade, a grade
  down after a miss (no similar order after a miss in the placement: on to the next strand), one
  grade at a time and **never above grade 4** (no decimals, no grade 5 from the placement). She then
  starts **one grade below** what she showed, for confidence (multiplication never below grade 3);
  skills of a strand below that are **assumed**: they are not taught first, but come now and then
  (about one order in five while there are some, gently at first) to collect their evidence -- a
  skill still counts as passed only by the usual rules. Setting the starting grade (setup tab, 2-5,
  default 3) places every strand there. It looks like any first game: no screen of its own.
- **The first sessions ramp slowly** (150 orders): nothing comes above her strand's grade (goals and
  reviews), the 85 % controller errs easy (seven rights in a row to step a difficulty up, a step down
  after three answers under 80 %, a ceiling of difficulty 1 for the first 60 orders, 2 until 150), and
  the quick-rights step obeys the same ceiling. **Decimals and grade 5** wait until their grade-4 base
  is passed with mastery 80+ and she is past her first two play days (`mq_skill_late_ok`), unless the
  grown-ups set her grade there. `test_new_player` plays a typical 9-year-old (good at facts, shaky on
  fractions and decimals) through three sessions: no decimal or grade-5 order, difficulty 0-1 in the
  first session, grade 3 at most after the placement.
- **The strand's own controller** (the audit's D2: the 85 % one moves a skill only after six answers of
  it): her last seven first answers in each strand; under 70 % over five or more steps the strand down
  a grade at once (the skills below become goals again), seven rights in a row step it up (after the
  first sessions, never above 4 on its own); goals never come more than a grade above the strand.
- **Her current goals**: the skills not passed whose prerequisites she knows (passed, mastery 60+,
  or assumed), the lowest grade first, those she has started before new ones (the facts weigh in
  while multiplication is on); the operations mix (never the same skill twice in a row, rarely the
  same strand); the place's own skills (+22) and the round's game (+18..24 for a skill whose game it
  is, +8 for a pick game) weigh in -- the bakery prefers fractions and measurement stories, Bubble
  Pop what pops well -- but any due skill comes to any place, told in the round's game when it fits,
  else in a variant of its own (the order is retold in its own game for that one order).
- **Moving quickly**: the first answers of a skill all right first time count +25 mastery (not +15),
  an assumed skill starts at difficulty 2 (1 in her first sessions), and three quick right first answers in a row (quicker
  than 60 % of her think time) step the difficulty up at once -- being fast moves the *maths* on
  (bigger numbers, the next step), never the speed of the game. The 85 % controller and the
  interleaved review run as before, beside it.

A test plays a new player who only ever plays one favourite game (Bubble Pop at the Garden's first
stop) for as long as it takes: every standard of grades 2-4 and every fact is passed in the end
(about 2,300 rounds of 5 orders), grade-4 and grade-5 skills appear in that one game, the difficulty
rises in nearly every skill played often, and the placement lands a grade-4 player at grade 4 and a
grade-3 player at grade 3 (`test_engine`).

## Pacing: the game fits the problem

Every order carries an **expected think time** (`mq_think_ms`): a model from its skill, difficulty,
the answer's digits, steps and the story's reading length, calibrated by her own first-answer times
for that skill and difficulty (a moving average in the save, `mq_pace_t.think`; a long pause is
clipped, so a chat or a snack does not slow everything). **Heavy** orders (column sums, long products
and sharing, multi-step stories, a long model think time) go to a calm game of their own -- the
heist's lock, the till, building, the cooking amounts -- when they have one (`mq_route_mech`); in a
moving game they **hover** (at most 16 % of the base speed). The answers of Bubble Pop and Cloud Catch
move at the game's speed for this order (`mq_speed_now`): her speed level for the game, capped so that
nothing escapes before **1.5 x the expected think time**, and never above a comfortable cruise (the base
speed). Nothing done for five seconds hovers them; an escape never costs anything (the bubble comes
round again, the star floats back up) but slows the game.

**The speed controller** (`mq_speed_event`, per player and per game, saved): a new player starts gently
(level 30 of 100); a miss, or a right answer that escaped her, lowers it by 20 % at once; a slow answer
by 5 %; quick rights raise it 5 % a step from the second in a row on (hysteresis: right-wrong-right-wrong
only ever goes down, then sits). It is separate from the maths difficulty (the 85 % controller): a miss
because the maths is hard slows the game and the maths controller reacts too. Pure speed fun lives only
in the optional arcade.

**Cloud Catch** (the falling-star game she found fast and confusing): three answer stars side by side
(never overlapping) come down together and **wait mid-air** until she moves her cloud; only a star she
went for lands on it; after a dud the stars wait again (calm); a missed right star floats back up; while
an order waits nothing else falls (no rocks, no clutter), and a rock between orders is a wobble, never a
lost star. The answers are stars with their numbers on them, and the catcher is a big cloud with her buddy
holding up a basket over her head (the pet rides along); the first-time demo slides a ghost cloud under a
ghost star with no number on it, which drops into the basket (never pointing at the answer). **Star Catch** (the arcade): the goal in a banner at the top ("Type a star's answer to catch
it!"), two big stars apart, a gentle fall (8 px a second, +1 a streak step) capped by 1.5 x her think time
for the fact; a star that lands floats back up and is not counted (it used to count as a slow right);
after a miss or six idle seconds the stars wait.

`test_pacing` checks every skill and difficulty in Bubble Pop and Cloud Catch (the time to escape at least
1.5 x the think time, heavy orders hovering or routed, never past the cruise), Cloud Catch's waiting stars,
the controller (a miss lowers it, quick rights raise it slowly to the cap, alternation never raises it, it
is kept with her player, idle hovers), fluency moving the maths on, and Star Catch.

## What to do, and help when it is hard

`mq_guide.c`; all in the round, in character, with no screen of its own.

- **The first-time demo**: the first order of every game (each game once per player: `mq_pace_t.demo_seen`)
  a ghost hand shows the gesture -- a sweep across the bubbles, a drag of the cloud, a tap on a gate,
  the card slid to a bin, the corner of the flower bed dragged, a hand turned round the clock -- while
  the place's guide says the goal in a bubble (and aloud on the Mac). Any input ends it; afterwards the
  goal banner is the short reminder. The demos point at every answer alike: they never show which.
- **Idle hints**: nothing done for five seconds (`q->last_input`) -- every target pulses (all the answers,
  or the game's go button) and the guide pops up in the corner pointing at the field ("Psst!", the goal
  said once).
- **Consistent controls and "what do I do?"**: every game is a tap or a drag plus its go button at the
  same place (bottom right), the arrows and ENTER do the same; the soft key F3 "help?" and the "?" in the
  top line replay the demo.
- **The lightbulb** (F1 "show me"): the situation again -- the story acted with its operation: the groups
  counted off one by one, one of the pile's things hopping to each plate in turn (a deal, never a count),
  the second pile sliding over to the first, a part lifting off -- and the operation's little tag. The tag
  ("3 groups, 4 each", "24 shared by 4", "put together", "take away", "3 times as many", "all the way
  around", ...: `mq_op_cue`) is also on the order board's row where it fits and in every acted story. It
  never holds the answer (tested for every skill with a story; `test_no_giveaway`'s rule holds for the
  bulb's frames too). Orders without a story stage show their picture as asked and their words.
- **The helper friend**: two misses on an order -- or a third slip of the same skill within six orders,
  at the first miss -- bring a friend: they hop, fly or pop in with a line of their own ("I'll help!",
  "Hoo! Let's look closely."), then walk through a similar order with fresh numbers one step a tap (the
  guide's worked example, the story acted first; a calm 6 s a step if she waits), then "Your turn!" and
  she solves her own order with the friend cheering beside her. Right after help: "Teamwork!", 3 hearts
  with that friend and the bounce-back coins (`mq_pace_t.teamwork`). Missed again: **together** -- the
  duds fade and the friend points at the one left, or the game is set to the answer (the bed, the clock,
  the pie, the line, the map, the lock) and the go button glows; games without either are told as a
  bubble for that order. So an order always ends in a success. Her favourite (cast slot 0) comes every
  other time, the others in turn (`mq_helper_pick`). Each helper moment is logged per skill
  (`mq_pace_t.helped`, the play log), brings that skill's review due within minutes, and the misses are
  counted by misconception (`mq_pace_t.mc_seen`). The placement's orders skip the helper (why, then the
  next strand). The trick of old is still there before a stop (its intro) and on request.

`test_guide` (drawn and spoken through the kid-word checker): every game's demo on a new player's first
round and not on the second, input ending it, the idle reminder, help, the bulb for every skill (nothing
countable equal to the answer) and back to the game, an operation tag for every story without the answer,
the helper's triggers and rotation, and the together guarantee in all 16 games.

## Talking characters

Every line a character says goes through `mq_speak` (and `mq_voice` for the old blips): the **speak hook**
(`modx_api_t.speak(who, text)`, API size 8, appended: `who` is the cast slot, visitors 9..16, with
`MODX_SPEAK_WORDS` to read it in words and `MODX_SPEAK_NOW` to interrupt); a no-op on the calculator,
logged by the simulator (state keys `modx_speak`, `modx_speaks`), forwarded by the web build to the page's
`calcSpeak(who, text)`. They speak the order's few words as it arrives (the whole story when the voices read
words), cheers, oops lines, the helper's steps, the guide's goal, town chatter and the friends' stories. On
the calculator (no speaker) the line types out in its bubble and the speaker's mouth goes (`AN_TALK` while it
types: `mq_t.say_t`). The grown-ups' setup has **Voices: cute babble (default) / words / off**
(`mq_pace_t.voice`); off leaves the captions only.

**On the Mac** (`web/page/sparkle_voice.js`): **cute babble** by default -- an "animalese" voice made in Web
Audio from the line itself: a formant blip a syllable (a syllable is a vowel group of a word; its vowel picks
the two formants), syllable-timed, with a pitch contour from the punctuation (up on "?", bouncy on "!", down
on "."), in each character's profile: a base pitch, a formant tint, a speed, a warble and a signature (a
squeak, a deep soft hum, a breathy puff, a chirp, a bell, a bounce). `SparkleVoice.plan(text, profile)` is
deterministic (tested). **Words** (reading help) use the Web Speech API with the most child-friendly installed
voice (an Enhanced / Premium one of Samantha, Karen, Moira, Tessa, Allison, Ava, ... first), the pitch raised to
1.1-1.5 and the rate a touch slower per character; without speechSynthesis it babbles. One line at a time: a
line marked "now" interrupts, others queue (two at most). The old blips (`MODX_SFX_VOICE`) play only when no
line came with them. A theme manifest may give each character a `"voice"` (`{"babble": {...}, "tts": {...}}`,
see `tools/make_theme.py`); `make_theme.py --voices FILE` writes them for the page (`SPARKLE_CONFIG.voices`).

## Curriculum and coverage

`mq_std[]` (mq_curric.c) has every grade-3 and grade-4 CCSS standard (3.OA.1-9,
3.NBT.1-3, 3.NF.1-3, 3.MD.1-8, 3.G.1-2, 4.OA.1-5, 4.NBT.1-6, 4.NF.1-7, 4.MD.1-7,
4.G.1-3: 53 standards), seven grade-2 foundations and every grade-5 standard (5.OA.1-3,
5.NBT.1-7, 5.NF.1-7, 5.MD.1-5, 5.G.1-4: 26),
each with its grade, its Star Math / Core Progress domain (NO numbers and operations, AT
algebraic thinking, FD fractions and decimals, GE geometry, MD measurement and data), a
description and the skills that teach it. Adaptive assessments such as Star Math reach
into neighbouring grades, which is why the foundations and the challenge items are
included; the game does not imitate a test (no timed quizzes, no question screen): the content
appears in the worlds' stories and games.

A **skill** (`S_*`) is a problem generator plus its worked example. A standard is
**passed** when all its skills are passed; a skill is passed at mastery 75 with at least
5 first-try answers (mastery +15 per first-try answer, +6 after help, -10 per miss);
3.OA.7's skill (Fact garden) is passed only when every required fact is fluent.
**Finishing the game** means every standard of grades 2-4 passed and every required
fact fluent (`mq_quest_complete`), celebrated with a crown.

`tests/unit/test_mathquest.c` fails `make test` if a required standard id is missing (all of
grades 3, 4 and 5 and the foundations), has no description or skill, a skill is not on a level
or has no game, a problem of a skill cannot be told in any game, a domain lacks grade-3/4 rows,
or a grade-5 skill is outside the two grade-5 worlds. The console `mq coverage` runs the
coverage check on the calculator. The grown-ups' screen says the grade she is working at.

## Fact fluency

Every multiplication fact a x b (a, b = 0..12) and division fact (a*b) / b
(b = 1..12) is tracked on its own: times seen, times right, a moving average of the
response time (0.1 s units) and a **Leitner box** 0..5. Required: 0..10 (231 facts);
the parent screen adds 11 and 12 (325).

- A quick right answer moves the fact up a box, a right but slow answer keeps it,
  a miss puts it in box 1 and asks it again two questions later. **Quick** is timed from the
  moment the answer can be given (after the acted story) to the input that gives it (a caught
  star's flight is not hers), and is within 3 s or within 90 % of her own usual time for facts in
  the games (a running average, `mq_pace_t.fact_t10`), at most 6 s (`mq_fact_quick_ms`): a
  game's answers fly in, and 3 s from the order's start was out of reach, so box 4 never came.
- Box k is due again 0, 1, 2, 4, 7, 12 fact rounds later (a round = one fact game or a
  level that drills facts).
- **Fluent** = box 4 or more, an average time of 6 s or less, **and box 4 held on two
  different play days** (the parity of the play day when box 4 was first reached is kept in the
  learning block, `fact_par`; bit 7 of `right` marks a fact confirmed on a later day). A fact
  learnt in one sitting is not yet a fact known: spaced evidence, like a mastered skill. Finishing
  the game needs every required fact fluent. In play, facts come as errands ("Pip needs 7 x 6
  strawberries for the party!"), as Peekaboo's "find the friend whose trays make 42", and on the
  beat in Bongo Beats.
- Next fact: a queued one (fact families, re-asks), else a due fact from the lowest box,
  else a new fact in teaching order of the factors 0, 1, 2, 10, 5, 3, 4, 9, 6, 7, 8,
  11, 12 (at most 6 new per round), else one that is not fluent yet. The rules check first
  asks just two facts: one x0 and one x1 (each with a factor of 2..9); after it x0 / x1 facts
  are at most 5 % of the facts. `test_mq_factrun.c` plays 30 play days of the whole game as a
  competent player, from a fresh save and from an old one with stalled boxes: x6, x7 and x8 come
  within the first weeks, x0 / x1 stay under 5 % in every 60 facts, and box 4 is reached.
- **Fact families** are practised together: a new or missed 3 x 7 queues 7 x 3, 21 / 3
  and 21 / 7.
- **Strategies** for the hard facts, shown on a miss and in worked examples, each with
  an array split where it helps: doubles (x2), double-double (x4), double three times
  (x8), half of ten groups (x5), ten groups minus one group (x9), double plus a group
  (x3), five groups plus one (x6), split 5 + 2 (x7), ten groups plus one or two (x11,
  x12), and "think multiplication" for division.
- The parent screen shows a 13 x 13 **fact grid** for x and for / (green fluent, yellow
  almost, pink learning, grey not yet).

## Mistakes and help: moments of the story

Every generator lists the common wrong answers of its problem and the misconception behind each
(`wrong[]` / `wrong_mc[]`; for a choice, each wrong tile carries one). The classifier
(`mq_classify`) maps a wrong answer to its misconception: a known wrong answer (fractions: also
an equivalent one), else a near miss ("so close"), else the generator's most likely one. In a
pick game the duds are exactly these known wrong answers (then near misses), so a dud picked is a
misconception recognised. Examples:

| Problem | Wrong answer | Named as |
|---|---|---|
| 47 + 38 | 75 | The ten did not get carried |
| 402 - 157 | 355 | Took the small digit from the big one |
| 603 - 278 | 335 | Borrowing across a zero (the 0 tens became 10, not 9) |
| 1/8 ? 1/4 | > | Bigger bottom number, bigger slice? |
| a rug of 24 squares, 4 tall | 8 wide (perimeter 24) | Area and perimeter mixed up |
| a fence of 20 m, 4 deep | 16 wide | Only two sides were added |
| the clock at 3:40 | 8:15 | Hour and minute hands swapped |
| 2:45 + 30 minutes | 2:75 | An hour is 60 minutes, not 100 |
| 39 friends, vans of 4 | 9 vans | The leftovers need one more |
| 0.5 ? 0.45 | < | A longer decimal is not bigger |
| 2/6 + 3/6 | 5/12 | Added the bottom numbers too |
| (3, 5) on the map | (5, 3) | x and y swapped |
| 3 x (4 + 5) | 17 | Did the brackets wait their turn |
| 3/4 x 12 rabbits | more | Times always makes bigger |

**A dud is a moment in the story** (`mq_story_oops`): the friend pouts and says what the answer
would mean, with its numbers where the story allows ("5 each leaves 4 on the plate. Mocha still
pouts!", "Oh no! 3 friends have no seat yet!", "That's the inside, not the fence around!"), the
dud bubble wilts, and she tries again. A second miss shows **why** in the game: the right answer
with the picture (the model, now with nothing hidden), and a similar order follows (fresh
numbers, one step easier); the skill comes back five orders later. Nothing is lost.

**The guide's trick** (`mq_steps`): a short show in the round (F1 "trick", offered after two
misses in a row, or before a new stop from its intro), its steps advancing by themselves or by a
tap, the model revealed in stages; it ends with a similar order (or the last step left for her:
a completion problem, while a skill is half learnt).

The old question screen (the problem, an answer pad, the feedback page) is kept only as the

**grown-ups' preview** (tap a standard on the parent screen, or `mq preview S`): normal play
never enters it, and a unit test plays every world, every finale, rights, wrongs, tricks and fun
beats to check that.

## Error patterns: her wrong answers over time

`mq_diag.c`. One wrong answer gets a name (the classifier above); a **pattern** is the same kind of
wrong answer coming back. A library of patterns, each knowing when an order gives it a chance to show
and whether her answer showed it:

| Pattern | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Forgets to carry into the tens / the hundreds / the thousands (three patterns: she may carry into the tens and never into the hundreds) | the order's own numbers (`mq_prob_t.ws`, `op`): her answer is the sum with that column's carry dropped |
| Takes the smaller digit from the larger | the digit-by-digit difference |
| Trades across a zero wrongly | the 0 made 10, not 9 (one ten too many) |
| Place-value slips | a zero lost or added, x 10 or / 10 |
| Adds the denominators, bigger denominator = bigger piece, the clock's hands swapped, an hour as 100 minutes, the graph's key skipped, skip counting a step off, the remainder ignored, coin values confused (a nickel taken for more than a dime: a new known wrong answer of *Count coins*), area and perimeter mixed, x and y swapped, a longer decimal is bigger | exactly one of the generator's known wrong answers with that misconception (never the classifier's fallback guess) |

**The evidence** is a log-likelihood ratio per pattern (`mq_diag_rec_t.score`, x 16 in log2 units) of "she
has it" against "her slips are random", per chance it had to show: shown, + log2(3 / c), where c is the chance
a random slip lands on it (typed or made answers 1/32; a pick game, the share of its wrong options that show
it); right, - 23; another wrong answer, log2(0.5 / (1 - c)). It is **spotted** at 160 (odds of 1024 to 1) with
at least 3 of its last 8 chances showing it and twice as many as other slips there. Mashing and guess mode
are no evidence; the placement and the grown-ups' preview neither.

**Then, in the story.** The miss that spots it (or the next one showing it) brings the friend: "Look! Yours
is 33, it is 43. Look at the tens!" -- her answer under the right one, column by column, the tens ringed in
both, then the step replayed (the ones: 7 + 6 makes 13, write the 3; the 1 hops up to the tens, a little
yellow box flying over; the tens: 3 + 0 + 1 makes 4), or for a trade the top digit crossed and the ten
moved, or for the clock, the pie, the graph her answer and the right one beside the picture with nothing
hidden, and two lines on what to look at. "got it!" and they finish the order together. Then **aimed
orders**: the engine asks the skill where it showed, generated until the order can show it (a carry into
the hundreds, a zero to trade across), at most one order in three (`mq_diag_pick`, `mq_diag_fits`); three
right and it goes to **re-checks** after 10, 20 and 40 orders; three passed and it is **fixed**. Showing it
again in a re-check sends it back to practice; her rights wearing the evidence down send it to re-checks;
a fixed pattern that comes back is spotted again. The friend shows it again at most every 8 orders.

**The grown-ups**: the Skills tab's "patterns" button lists every pattern seen (watching, practice n/3,
re-check n/3, fixed), shown of its chances, and her last example ("37+6: 33, not 43"); the play log has a
row per pattern. `mq diag [show PT|slip PT|give V|next|done|reset]`;
`tests/unit/test_mq_diag.c` (the detectors on worked examples; synthetic players following each pattern,
typed and picked, are diagnosed in 3 to 12 of its chances -- up to 25 when every wrong option shows it --
and nothing else is; 24 random slippers, 14,000 orders, 70-94 % right, typed and picked: no diagnosis;
the moment, the aimed orders, the re-checks to fixed; every pattern's moment drawn without school words),
`tests/ui/49_mathquest_diag.txt` (screenshots `mq_diag_*.png`).

## Theme packs

A theme pack replaces the cast's pictures and names (and optionally world colours)
without changing the game. Packs live on the card as `/CALC/THEMES/NAME.THM` (8.3
names), are chosen on the parent screen (setup) and are loaded again at every start; a
missing card, a missing file, a damaged file (CRC) or another version leaves the
built-in cast (the setup page says why).

Making one: draw the characters in a PNG sheet of equal cells (at most 48 x 48 pixels; 32 x 32 for an older Math Quest;
transparent background), write a manifest and run

    python3 tools/make_theme.py --sheet sheet.png --manifest theme.json --out PASTEL.THM

```json
{
  "title": "Pastel friends",
  "cell": [20, 20],
  "sprites": [
    {"cell": 0, "slot": "garden", "name": "Pip"},
    {"cell": [1, 0], "slot": "post", "name": "Dot"}
  ],
  "worlds": {"garden": {"background": "#e8faee", "accent": "#5cbf8a", "ground": "#a8e6cf"}}
}
```

Slots: 0 garden, 1 post, 2 bakery, 3 clock, 4 shop, 5 art, 6 fair, 7 racer (the arcade
friend), 8 sky. Missing slots keep the built-in characters. Slot 0 is the player's best friend:
the stories pick it (or the world's guide) as the friend being helped most of the time. YAML manifests work when
PyYAML is installed. `python3 tools/gen_mq_art.py --sheet DIR` writes the built-in cast as
a sheet and manifest to start from; `make_theme.py --info FILE` lists a pack.

**Animated characters**: a pack may give each cast slot animation frames -- idle (2-4: a
slow loop, breathing, a blink), happy (2-4: the jump on a right answer), think (2: the "hmm" on
a miss), dance (4: the end of a run), pout (2: a little sulk in a story) and fly (2-4: aloft) -- as extra sprites with `"anim"` and `"frame"` in the
manifest. The frames go in the sprite entries' spare bytes, so an animated pack is still version
1 (an older Math Quest shows each slot's first sprite, still). An animation without frames is
made from the slot's idle frame 0 with the same moves as the built-in cast (bob and blink, jump
with squash and stretch, a tilt with a "?", lean-squash-lean-stretch): `mq_sprite_anim`.

**Characters for the stories** (version 3): a manifest's `"characters"` names each slot's
favourite food, colour, hobby and thing (singular nouns, at most 15 characters; plurals are made)
and a pronoun (she, he, they). The stories then star the pack's own characters doing what they
like ("Marshmallow bakes 3 cinnamon buns for Bubblegum..."). A pack with traits but a slot
without them uses generic words for it.

**Sprite variants** (version 3): `"variants"` adds the same sprite drawn for another display scale
(2x to 8x, RGB565), for a high-resolution platform; a 1x display reads past them.

Theme packs may also redraw **home items** (version 2): a manifest lists `"items"`, each a
catalogue key (`gen_mq_items.py --list`) or id with a rectangle or cell of the sheet (up to 64
x 64 pixels, 48 items); the picture replaces the built-in one everywhere (room, tray, shop,
book) at 2x, bottom-centred on the item's footprint (half size if it is bigger).
`gen_mq_items.py --sheet DIR` writes every item as a template sheet (`items.png`) and a
manifest of the first 48. A pack without items is written as version 1, which every Math
Quest reads; this module reads all three. A version-3 pack is refused by an older Math Quest
(it keeps its own cast), so make_theme.py writes version 3 only when the manifest has
characters or variants.

Format (little-endian):

| Offset | Size | Field |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | magic `MQTH` |
| 4 | 2 | version (1; 2 with home items; 3 with characters or variants) |
| 6 | 2 | header size (64) |
| 8 | 24 | title, NUL-padded |
| 32 | 1+1 | cell width, height (1..32) |
| 34 | 1 | sprites (1..160, 112 before this module version; version 2: 0..160; at most 368,640 pixels in all) |
| 35 | 1 | flags: bit 0 = world skins present; bit 1 = characters (v3); bit 2 = variants (v3) |
| 36 | 2 | colours (1..256; index 0 is transparent) |
| 38 | 2 | home items (version 2; 0 in version 1) |
| 40 | 4 | payload bytes |
| 44 | 4 | CRC-32 (zlib) of the payload |
| 48 | 16 | 0 |
| 64 | | payload: palette (colours x u16 RGB565); sprites x {char name[12], u8 slot, u8 anim (0 idle, 1 happy, 2 think, 3 dance), u8 frame, u8 0}; if flag 0: 8 worlds x {u16 background, accent, ground} (0 = built-in); pixels: sprites x width x height u8 palette indices, row by row; version 2: items x {u16 item id, u8 width, height (1..64)}, then their pixels (width x height each); unknown ids are skipped; version 3, flag 1: u8 n, n x {u8 slot, u8 pronoun (1 she, 2 he, 3 they), char food[16], colour[16], hobby[16], thing[16]}; flag 2: u16 n, n x {u8 sprite, u8 scale, u16 w, h, w x h u16 RGB565 (0x0001 transparent)} |

The module keeps the loaded pack's sprites run-length coded in its RW block (at most 72 KB coded:
e.g. 140 frames of 40 x 40, enough for talk and wow faces for every friend; make_theme.py checks it) and decodes a few at a time. Newer animations:
talk (the mouth going), wow, look (frames 0 and 1: looking left, then right, an idle glance) and
blink (with a blink frame the idle frames are not cycled as a blink; the engine blinks every 3 s). `make test` builds a pack from the built-in cast with `make_theme.py` and
checks that the module loads it pixel for pixel (`mq-tools-test`).

## Why it plays like this (design rationale)

The game is meant to pull a child back day after day and to teach while it does; the choices
below come from learning-science and game-design research and are all invisible to the child.
Most are in `mq_coach.c`; "tested" means `tests/unit/test_mathquest.c` measures it.

| Choice | How | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Success near 85 % | each skill's difficulty follows its last 8 first-try answers: above 90 % one step harder, below 75 % one step easier (tested: a simulated player settles at 78-92 %) | Wilson, Shenhav, Straccia & Cohen 2019, "The Eighty Five Percent Rule for optimal learning", *Nature Communications* |
| Try again once | a dud gets one more try, the friend saying what it would mean in the story; then why, in the game, and the trick after two misses; a sort into two bins (or the last two bubbles) gets no second try, which would be no thinking at all (tested) | Shute 2008, "Focus on formative feedback"; Hattie & Timperley 2007 |
| Faded scaffolding | concrete then representational then abstract: the question's model hides countable units unless counting is the skill, and drops away once a skill is mastered; worked examples are full while a skill is new (mastery < 40), a completion problem in the middle (she finishes the example's last step herself) and not offered once mastered (tested) | Renkl & Atkinson 2003 (fading); Kalyuga et al. 2003 (expertise reversal); Fyfe et al. 2014 (concreteness fading) |
| Production still counts | most orders are picked in a game now (a pop, a catch, a gate), but typing the answer is always allowed and picks it (her own answer), many games build the answer (frost, plant, drag a rug, turn the hands, drop coins, aim, hop), and the vault, the till and the speed rounds are typed; the duds are the known misconceptions, so a pick is diagnostic | Roediger & Karpicke 2006 (production vs recognition); Habgood & Ainsworth 2011 (intrinsic integration) |
| Interleaving | earlier skills are mixed into every run (the learning engine above); "which operation?" story moments make her choose x, /, + or - | Rohrer & Taylor 2007; Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic 2015 |
| Spaced evidence for mastery | a skill counts as mastered only when its bar is reached again on a later play day; facts need four Leitner promotions in different rounds (tested) | Cepeda et al. 2006 (spacing); Leitner 1972 |
| Fraction magnitude | the number line and comparing fractions get extra weight in the interleaved review; placing fractions on a line is its own level | Siegler, Thompson & Schneider 2011; Siegler & Lortie-Forgues 2014 |
| Speed without pressure | no timer on any learning moment; response times are measured silently for fluency; speed lives in the arcade, where time never makes her lose | Boaler 2014 (timed tests and anxiety) |
| Guessing | two quick wrong first answers (< 1.5 s) in a row: the next three items earn nothing and number tiles become typed answers, quietly (tested) | Baker et al. 2004 (gaming the system) |
| Self-determination | autonomy: after a run she picks what's next (next stop, arcade, home); competence: personal records, stars; relatedness: the guides greet her by name and remember her streak, show-off mode | Ryan & Deci 2000; Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006 |
| Intrinsic integration | the maths IS the action: every order is played in a game (no question screen); fun streams between the orders | Habgood & Ainsworth 2011 |
| Stories with a motive | orders are errands for the cast with a mild feeling (a pout, a worry, a hurry) and a happy ending; misconceptions are told as what they would mean in the story | Cordova & Lepper 1996 (personalised contexts) |
| Freshness | a weekly event by play days (Blossom, Beach, Pumpkin, Snow week) with a seasonal item in the shop; new friends visit as worlds open | Hamari et al. 2014 (novelty) |
| The first five minutes | no tutorial screens: a new player is in the first adventure at once, with the game's goal shown in place ("Pop the bubble that fits!"), wins in about two minutes and gets a squishy | Gee 2005 (just-in-time learning) |
| Low reading load | short sentences, icons, large type; colours that carry meaning stay apart for protanopes and deuteranopes (tested with the Vienot, Brettel & Mollon 1999 simulation), and meaning never rests on colour alone | WCAG 2.2, 1.4.1 |
| Grown-ups | conversation prompts from her current world ("Ask her: how many quarters make a dollar?"), the fact grid, standards, review statistics, a play log (per-skill success, first-answer times, long thinks, where play stopped) on the screen, in `mq log`, and as `/CALC/MQLOGnnn.CSV` on the card (never sent anywhere) | |
| Healthy play | no punishment for stopping or missing days (the gift streak just starts again), natural stopping points after each run, a cosy "the cafe closes for the night" when the daily limit is reached, no fake scarcity, no loot boxes, no pressure messages; engagement is measured only for the grown-ups (time, sessions) | |

## Fun beats, squishies and juice

`mq_fun.c`. Playing the maths fills an invisible budget of pure fun: about 10 s per 90 s of maths
(the grown-ups' "free fun share", 0-30 %, default 10 %), capped at 20 s so it is never hoarded.
At natural moments (a streak of 5, the end of a run, a finale won, or when play gets hard: two
misses in a row or a very slow answer) a short **fun beat** is slipped in when the budget has at
least 6 s and at least 3 maths moments passed since the last one (never two in a row): a jam with Puff the drum sheep (the toy box); bubbles to pop (touch, or the number keys by column) -- each time one of three
little games on them, as play with nothing kept: **count by** 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s or 10s (pop them in order),
**make ten** (or a hundred: pop two that do) and **the bigger slice** (fraction bubbles two by two); a tap out
of turn only wobbles (`BB_*`, `mq_bubbles_begin`); **Catch the Stars** (see below); a dance to tap along to; a story moment of the world's chapter; a squishy
arriving; the squishy maker; trying on a new outfit; placing a new thing. (Pet time -- a pet to pat and feed -- was cut: the user found it lame; its slot `BEAT_GONE0` stays
reserved.) Beats rotate and never
repeat back to back, and the ones that carry numbers (bubbles, the catch) come round three times as often as each other one; each lasts 8-10 s (or until the budget runs out; Catch the Stars: its whole count, as maths time), then the story carries on
with the next maths moment. Her room and dress-up, opened herself, spend the same budget (no beats then until
maths fills it again; the shop and the book spend none); a long stay at home is the home story's (below: a friend
who needs her, at a calm moment) -- no meter, no label, no blocking message. A
little budget (15 s) is there at every start, so decorating what she owns is always possible.

**Catch the Stars: what it teaches.** The catch beat is skip-counting, the root of the times table, with the
answer as the target -- not a reflex game. The banner gives the count so far and asks for the next number
("Count by 3s: 6, 9, ?", or "Count back by 4s: 52, 48, ?"); the star to catch is the next number (12), found
among the count's other numbers (the one just passed, the ones further on) and the numbers between them (11, 15,
13). A star that is not next bounces off gently (nothing lost, no miss counted) and the count goes on from where
she is. **A round is a long stretch of the count** (the user: "ends too quickly to really practice the skill"):
10 to 12 catches, shown as a row of little stars that light as she catches them, from a varied start (0 to 3
times the step) so the count always goes well past ten times (by 3s from 6: ... 36, 39); about one round in
three counts **back** from grade 3 (down to the step itself, never 0); the end says what she did ("You counted
by 3s to 39!"). The steps: 2, 5, 10, 3, 4 (the order the tables are met), from grade 4 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, and half
the time the table she is learning (the times-table runs). The stars fall slowly at one speed that never ramps
up, and the next number always comes soon (a star carrying it is falling most of the time), so the work is
finding the number, not racing a hand. It is maths time, not the fun budget's: it lasts until the count is done,
and ends by itself when she leaves the basket alone for 25 s. `test_catch_stars` (test_mathquest.c) plays whole
rounds: 10-12 catches past ten times, one fall speed, wrong stars gentle, the start varied, counting back only
from grade 3, at least 20 s a round, and the next number falling most of the time.

**Squishies**: eight kawaii shapes (bun, peach, cat, cloud, mochi, bear, strawberry, star), four
colours and a rare sparkly variant (a strawberry is always red -- red, deep red, pink, light red -- with black seeds
and a green leafy top, a peach always peachy; the others any of four pastels; the name says the colour: "pink strawberry",
"blue cat"; `mq_squish_colour`, `mq_squish_name`) (64 to collect, `mq_home_t.squish`), shown on a squishy shelf
(it comes with the first one) in the room. A squishy is drawn soft: its shade under a lighter body nudged toward the light, a glossy highlight
and shine, a two-pixel outline that follows the soft body all round, big shiny eyes and blush ovals, and
each kind's own touch (the bun's swirl, the peach's leaf, the cat's whiskers, the cloud's rainbow tuft, the
mochi's flour, the bear's muzzle, the strawberry's seeds and leaves, the star's twinkle). When it has risen
all the way after a squash it wobbles once (a decaying jiggle of its top), smiles ^ ^ and sparkles
(`mq_t.sq_rise_t`; on the Mac the vector art draws it from the same points, `MODX_HR_SPRITE`). A squishy is a
ring of 24 spring-mass points in integer Q8 (`mq_squish_step`): pressing squashes it (wider and lower, a dent under the finger, eyes
"> <"), dragging from the edge stretches it, a pinch squeezes it, and it slowly rises back to rest
with a little face. Streaks of 10 give squishy dough; two dough make a squishy of her choice in the
maker.

**Juice**: coins fly to the counter, a combo meter fills over five and bursts, the guide jumps
on a right answer and tilts "hmm" on a miss, the buddy dances at the end of a run, the finale
has a bouncing title and spinning spotlights, praise never repeats back to back, a curtain opens
on each new scene, the daily gift box opens with a flying lid. The gift grows with play days in a
row (up to five); a missed day just starts again, nothing is lost. The end of a run shows a
personal record (the longest streak) and what the coins could buy next. Frames stay at 25 fps
(`MQ_FRAME_MS`).

## The learning engine: problem histories and interleaved review

`mq_learn.c`; invisible to the child (an earlier skill just appears as the next item of the
game), shown on the grown-up screen (time tab: skills in review, due, the mix share, slips).

**Every problem has a history** (`mq_seen_t`, 12 bytes, 256 per profile): its canonical hash
(`mq_prob_ident`: the skill and its numbers, commutative twins alike), when it was last seen and
when it may come back (minutes of *play*: there is no clock), the last 8 results, the current
streak of rights and an SM-2 style ease (1.30..3.00). Every attempt is recorded. After a right
answer the problem stays away for base(mastery) x ease x 2^(streak-1) minutes of play (base =
10 + 2 x mastery; a slow right counts half and does not raise the ease); a miss lowers the ease
by 0.20, resets the streak and brings the problem back after 2 minutes (similar problems with new
numbers come in between). So a long clean streak puts a problem away for hours of play, one
right after many misses only briefly. `mq_generate_fresh` regenerates a candidate whose history
says "not yet" (tiny problem sets relax step by step and alternate). The entry seen longest ago
makes room for a new one. The x / / facts keep their Leitner boxes (one mechanism per kind).

**Interleaved review of earlier skills** (`mq_rev_t` per skill): a skill joins when first played
(box 1) and moves up when passed (box 3); box k is due again 5, 15, 45, 120, 360, 900 minutes of
play later. A share of every run's items (15 % at first; 10..40 %) comes from a due earlier
skill instead: the most overdue first, skills with weak problems that are due get priority, and
when the current skill is hard for her (a miss in the run, or low mastery) its prerequisites come
in even before they are due (`mq_prereq[]` in mq_curric.c: division -> multiplication, adding
fractions -> equivalent fractions, area -> counting squares, ...). A right answer moves the skill
up a box and the share down by 1 point; a wrong one moves it down a box, lowers its mastery a
step (15) and raises the share by 5; a second slip in a row brings the guide's trick. Review
items obey the no-repeat rules. The short "a missed skill comes back 5 problems later" review
of the profile stays.

**Save**: version 3 appended the learning block; version 4 (86 skills, grade 5 in full) grows
the per-skill arrays of the profile and the learning block; version 5 appends `mq_extra_t` per
profile (384 bytes: memories, hearts, story steps, the merge board, events, the new games' bests);
version 6 appends `mq_more_t` per profile (4 KB: the long memory of 160 events, the stage's pacing
counts, the visitors and festivals she has seen, 2.4 KB of it reserved). The whole save is
24,556 bytes (16,364 at version 5, 20 bytes short of the old 16 KB limit) of `MODX_SAVE_MAX` =
96 KB, so 73 KB can still be appended (a test keeps at least 64 KB free). The header's `size`
saturates at 65,535; the true length is the core's (`api->save_size`, API size 7). Versions 4
and 5 are prefixes of the layout and are read straight into the save (version 5's eight memories
move into the long memory, `mq_more_migrate`); versions 1-3 (71 skills) are read into scratch (the
theme's pixel pool, before any theme is loaded) and converted field by field (`save_from_v3`),
then migrated as before (a version-2 save: played skills join the review, the histories start
empty).

## Action games (the keypad)

`mq_action.c`, in the arcade below the four fact games; keys first, touch as well. Each game mixes
pure action with "grab the right number" moments, with nothing telling them apart:

| Game | Controls | Pure action | Number moments | Open |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud Dash | UP / DOWN switch lanes (or tap a lane); typing a number jumps to the lane that has it | dodge rocks, grab hearts and stars | a row of three answer bubbles: run through the right one | from the start |
| Star Sled | LEFT / RIGHT (or drag); typing a number steers under it | catch falling stars, dodge rocks | three numbers fall: catch the right one | from the start |
| Pop-up Pals | the number keys as they lie on the keypad (7 8 9 / 4 5 6 / 1 2 3) or a tap | bop the friends that pop up | numbers pop up: bop the right one | from the start |

The questions are the x / / facts from their scheduler (Leitner boxes, no repeats); each answer
is recorded like any fact. Number moments count as maths time and the pure stretches as fun time
for the invisible budget, which sets the rhythm: a pure stretch lasts a third of the fun budget
(3-8 s), so numbers come more often when it runs low. The action speed adapts on its own (a crash:
a step slower; a clean run: a step faster), apart from the maths difficulty. Nothing ends a game
early; it lasts 45 s and ends on the coins it earned; a personal best is saved ("New best!" and a trophy). The physics is integer and
stepped by frames (`mq step`), so tests are deterministic: collisions (`mq_hit`), lanes, the keypad
map, answering, crashes, the budget and the bests are unit-tested, and
`tests/ui/49_mathquest_action.txt` plays all three.

## Home: coins, the purse, the shop, rooms and collections

`mq_home.c`, `mq_purse.c`; the catalogue and its art come from `tools/gen_mq_items.py` (generated
`mq_items.c` / `mq_items.h`, `make mq-art`; `make test` fails if they are out of date).

**Coins, not points.** Coins are the one currency she earns and sees: every title line, the town's
bar, the round's top line and its end show **her purse** (a little pink pouch) with the balance
("55¢", "$3.20"; the cent sign is drawn: the fonts have none). Stars are no longer a score: the
round's "+1"s are flourishes ("yay!", "wow!"), a game's new best is "New best!" with a sparkle and
no number, the player cards show her purse. (The profile's star count stays as the unlock ladder's
quiet clock; nothing spends it.) **A game ends on its coins**: the arcade's games, the action games and
Bongo Beats end with the coins they earned showering into the purse ("+12¢", `mq_coins_end`); a best is a
little gold trophy (on the game's card in the arcade, on its world card and intro) and "New best!" with it,
never a number; the action games' pick-ups are hearts and stars (sparkles, never money: the maths earns the
coins) and their top line shows the coins of the game. `test_kid_screens` fails on a score on a kid screen
("12 stars!", "Best: 40", "best 7", "30 points").


**The purse** (`mq_purse.c`). The balance is kept in cents (`mq_home_t.purse`; a coin earned is a
cent: a right answer 3¢, a skill mastered 25¢; the shop's everyday things cost 4¢ to 50¢, its dear things $1 to $10). `coins` mirrors it,
capped at $600, so an older module still reads it, and whatever an older module earned or spent since
(`coins` no longer `purse_mirror`) is taken over at the next start. The balance is made of pennies,
nickels, dimes, quarters, $1 and $5 bills (`mq_home_t.hand`, always summing to it). Earnings drop
in as coins that make the reward (the coins flying into the purse are those: 37¢ is a quarter, a dime
and two pennies), and the handful is tidied (the fewest pieces) when it would hold more than 28
coins. **Before every purchase the handful is composed afresh, quietly** (never while she pays;
`mq_purse_compose`):

- the price can always be paid exactly, in the coins of her rung of *Count coins* (dimes and pennies;
  with nickels; with quarters; with dollars), now and then with a coin broken into smaller ones;
- for making change, the bill to pay over with is there too ($1 for a price under a dollar on the
  first rungs, $5 after);
- for the picky cashier, two ways to pay: a broken coin and the fewest coins;
- the rest of the balance as the fewest pieces: so at most 28 coins (never 200 pennies), the $5 bills
  apart as a stack ("x12").

**The fewest coins.** US coins and bills are a canonical system, so the greedy way is the fewest when
there are enough of each (`mq_coins_greedy`); from a given handful it is a small exact search
(`mq_coins_fewest`: 30¢ from a quarter and three dimes is the three dimes). `test_mq_purse` checks
both against brute force.

**Paying at the counter** (`mq_pay_mode`, `mq_pay_*`). "buy!" on an item's card:

| When | How she pays |
|---|---|
| before *Count coins* (not played, not placed above it) | a tap buys it, as always |
| *Count coins* reached (2.MD.8) | **the counter**: the cashier says the price ("That's 37¢!"); she drags or taps coins from her purse onto the counter, taps a counter coin to take it back (the arrows and ENTER do it too) and taps "pay!". The counter never shows a running total -- counting is hers |
| *Making change* reached, taught at school (4.MD.2), quarters solid, room in her purse | **paying over**, every other purchase: "A dollar bill is fine!"; she pays (more than one piece that is not needed: "Fewer will do"), then types the change on the counter's keys (`$0.75`, the digits shifting in like the till's). Exactly the price is fine too |
| exact paying solid (four right first time in a row) with quarters | **the picky cashier**, taking turns: "The fewest coins, please!", "3 coins at most!" or "No pennies today!" |

A wrong payment: a hint ("that's not 37¢ yet", "more than 37¢"); the second: **"help me pay"** by itself
-- the coins on the counter that are not in a working set slide back into her purse, the ones still to
put down are ringed with how many; the third: the cashier counts it out with her and it is bought. Wrong
change: "Count up from 37¢!", then counted out together. Nothing ever blocks buying: CLEAR puts the coins
back (and the counter closes on the next one), nothing is lost. **The picky cashier**: the fewest coins
earn **a bonus sparkle** (+3 kitchen energy and a dance); right but too many coins and he grumbles,

**Money practice.** Each purchase is one answer of *Count coins* or *Making change* for the engine: the
problem's history (`mq_seen_record`), interleaved review (joins; answers it when due), the success
window and her rung at the counter's own rung (`mq_coach_answer`, `mq_rung_answer`), the skill's
mastery and the strand controller. Help or a miss counts as a miss for the rung, so a rung never climbs
on help (no give-aways); the counter's coins come from her rung, so it eases when she struggles.

**Money's art.** Coins (copper penny, silver nickel, dime and quarter, the value on them), $1 and $5

## The home story: a friend who needs her

`mq_homestory.c`. Her room and dress-up are lovely and they are hers; but a kid who only moves furniture never
meets the maths. So the home keeps a quiet clock and, when she has been home a long while, the story moves -- gently,
inside the story, never as a timer, a meter, a lock or a punishment.

**The home time.** `mq_home_t.home_s` counts the seconds she spends in her room, dress-up and show-off (only while
she is there: a touch or a key in the last minute; never while she pays: paying is maths). The shop and the book
stop the clock. Every order played outside takes a minute back (the home time / 6, at least 30 s; a quick fact in
the arcade a third of that), a miss as much as a right answer; finishing a friend's errand sets it to nothing; a new
play day starts afresh. The grown-ups' Time tab sets it: off, 3, 6 (the default), 10 or 15 minutes.

**The escalation**, once the home time is used up:

| When | What she sees |
|---|---|
| used up | **restless**: her buddy wanders to the window (the room's window if one is up, else the door side) and looks out, a little picture in a bubble -- a droopy flower, a letter, a parcel, a stopped clock, a cake, a star; in dress-up a thought bubble over her buddy's head |
| 40 s later | **the hook**, at a calm moment (nothing touched for 1.5 s): a knock (two clicks), and a card at the top -- the friend's face and their little news ("A letter from Dot! Parcels to deliver! Can you help?", "Knock! It's Momo! Lots of customers!", "Pip waves outside! The flowers droop!"), **"go see!"** and **"later"**. "later" only closes it; "go see!" is the only thing on it that goes anywhere |
| a minute after that | **the walk-out**, at a calm moment (three seconds without a touch, no finger down, nothing dragged, not paying, no card of hers open): "Come on, let's go! Dot needs us!", her buddy heads for the door; any touch or key calls it off (it comes back at the next calm moment); after 2.5 s she is in the friend's place and its game, straight to the orders ("Dot: You came!") |

The friend and place come in turn (the next one each errand). **The errand** is a round in that place: finishing it
(that round, or any later round there, if she came home first) is the errand done -- the friend's **thank-you present**:
a decoration for her room (the cheapest she does not have, of that place's set first; it is already in her room,
shining, when she gets home) or a piece of clothing, in turn; a 25c tip when she has everything. The done screen says
"Thank you! A Stamp poster for you!" and its "home" button (lit) takes her straight back. Nothing is ever locked: she
can go home in the middle of the errand (the friend still waits), the store and the book are always there, and the
story never comes over the counter (CR-12), a drag, a dialog of hers, the grown-ups' gate or a fun beat.

**Why** (the learning reason). Play at home is the reward the maths pays for, and it should stay a reward, not a place
to hide: the coins that buy clothes and furniture come only from the town, so home play leans on outdoor play by
itself; the home time makes the hand-over between them part of the story instead of a rule. A 9-year-old who does not
love maths responds to being needed by a friend far better than to a timer -- the errand is a reason to go, the
thank-you ties the maths back to her room, and the refill (a minute per order) means a few orders buy a few minutes of
decorating: short, regular maths sessions spread over play, which is what spaced practice wants, without ever saying
so. The gentle steps (a look out of the window, a knock, then the buddy's own "let's go") give her the chance to go
by herself first; the calm-moment rule keeps it from ever feeling like being dragged away from something she is doing.

buying at the counter in dress-up, the old save), `tests/ui/49_mathquest_homestory.txt` (screenshots

## Fair play: no repeats, no give-aways

- **No repeats** (`mq_generate_fresh`): the last 10 problems per profile are remembered by
  skill and the numbers of the story and model (6 x 7 and 7 x 6 count as the same), the last
  3 also by answer and picture; a new problem that matches is generated again. Skills with
  only a handful of possible problems relax to "never twice in a row" and alternate. The
  similar problem after a mistake therefore always has new numbers. The fact scheduler keeps a
  fact and its twin away for the next 3 facts (re-asks and families included) unless nothing
  else is left.
- **The question's model never gives the answer away.** Each skill has `visual_may_reveal`
  (`mq_skill[]`): `VR_MAY` where reading or counting the model is the skill (clocks, coins,
  graphs, tables, the ruler, naming a shaded fraction, counting squares, shapes and angles,
  place-value charts, the coordinate map), `VR_NO` otherwise (`VR_LEVEL0`, dots of equal groups
  and unit cubes at difficulty 0, is no longer used: the story stage shows the groups with their
  labels instead, and the dots come after the answer). For `VR_NO` the play screen draws the
  model so that nothing can be counted for the answer: multiplication as a labelled box
  ("7 rows of 6"), division as one pile and empty pots, area and perimeter as the sides
  without unit squares, L-shapes without squares, sums and differences lined up by place,
  fraction pies and bars cut but not shaded, the unknown of an equation as "?", the change
  question as a till, the jug full, boxes without cubes, the next number of a pattern as "?".
  The full model appears in the feedback and the worked example, after the answer. A picture that holds
  the question's data (the coloured parts of pies, bars and the hundredths grid, graphs, clocks, coins) is
  drawn whole from the question's first frame (`mq_vis_data`: the pink cake). Weights and liquids are real
  things: a kitchen scale (a sack on the plate, a dial and its needle, "6 kg" under it), a balance (the
  thing on one pan, the beam tipping) and a measuring jug (a mark each liter, numbered as often as the
  numbers fit; a question numbers only the full mark).
  `test_no_giveaway_games` renders the opening frames of every game for every skill that is not
  `VR_MAY` (each of its games and Bubble Pop, every difficulty) and fails when the frame draws as
  many countable things as the answer (the card, the stage, the sort card, the pie, the till:
  `q->frame_units`).
  `MQ_DUMP=1 build/test/test_mathquest` lists a problem of every skill.

## The grown-ups' gate

(`mq_gate.c`; the user: "the grown-up gate is not going to keep 3rd graders out" -- it was a 2-second hold, and
"can't hold?" asked 7 x 8.) The way to the grown-ups' screen is **one problem just past the top of the
curriculum**, its answer typed. A grown-up does it in their head in a few seconds; a child the program is for
has not met it yet. A child who can do it has outgrown Sparkle Town -- and is welcome in.

**The rule.** `MQ_TOP_GRADE` (mq.h, 5 today) is the top grade Sparkle Town teaches. The gate asks only kinds
taught in the grades **above it, up to three above** (past the hardest kind there is: the hardest). When grade 6
is added, set `MQ_TOP_GRADE` to 6 and the gate lifts one notch by itself (the grade-6 kinds drop out;
`test_mq_gate.c` checks it). The kinds today, each phrased in plain grown-up words:

| Kind | Grade | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| a percent | 6.RP.3c | What is 15% of 80? (no 10 % or 50 %: short cuts a child may know) |
| powers in an expression | 6.EE.1 | What is 7 squared, plus 3 times 4? What is 2 to the power 5, minus 37? What is 4 cubed, minus 20? |
| negative numbers | 7.NS.1 | What is negative 8, plus 15, minus 20? What is 12 minus 30? |
| multiplying negatives | 7.NS.2 | What is negative 6 times 4, plus 30? What is negative 6 times negative 9, minus 46? |
| a two-step equation | 7.EE.4a | If 3n + 7 = 31, what is n? If 6n - 29 = 25, what is n? |
| square roots | 8.EE.2 | What is the square root of 144, plus 27? ... the square root of 225, minus the square root of 9? 2 times the square root of 289? |
| the letter on both sides | 8.EE.7 | If 5n - 4 = 2n + 11, what is n? |

**Generated, never a list.** Each problem is made from random numbers (the gate's own generator, so the game's
stay deterministic; reseeded from the clock each time it opens), its answer a whole number with
2 <= |answer| <= 99, and it is checked again from its own text in the test. Over 10 000 draws there are about
160 different answers and none comes up in more than 4 % (the rule: never 10 %); every kind comes up.

**Typed, not picked**: a pad of the shared widgets (1-9, 0, -, del, OK, close: tap, the arrows and ENTER for the
focus ring) and the keys (digits, (-) or -, DEL, ENTER answers, CLEAR or F5 closes; the Mac's digits, -, Backspace,
Return, Escape). A pick would be a guess.

**A wrong answer**: "Not quite." -- no hint, nothing of the answer shown, the pad off, then a new problem after a
pause that grows with the wrong answers in a row: 1.5 s, 3, 6, 12, 24, then **30 s at most** (never a lock-out).
Closing and opening it again does not skip a pause; a right answer clears the count, and a streak 10 minutes old is
forgotten. Nobody at the gate for 2 minutes: it closes. Taps, a finger held down (the old hold gate's CR2 bug: a
short tap is DOWN then TAP with no UP, and the gate opened 2 s later), drags, the wheel, swipes, typed letters and
the soft-key bar never open it.

**Every way in goes through it.** The grown-ups' screen opens only from a right answer (`open_parent` is called by
the gate, by the preview's way back, and by the console). The preview of a question (from the Skills tab) goes back
to the grown-ups' screen only when a grown-up used it in the last 5 minutes (else to her town), and the grown-ups'
screen left alone for 5 minutes goes back to her town. The Mac page's "Grown-ups..." only shows the gate (`mq gate`);
the page has no other way to the console a child can reach. Console commands are for tests and the bot only: `mq gate
[seed N | answer | pass | wait]` (fixed problems, the answer, through it as a right answer would, the pause) and
`mq go parent`. State keys: `mq_gate` (off / up / wait), `mq_gate_kind`, `mq_gate_text` (the problem shown,
never its answer).

**The limit (accepted).** On the calculator she can leave the game and use the calculator itself. The problems are
phrased in words ("negative 8", "squared", "the square root of", "15% of", "3n") rather than as something to type
straight in: to use the calculator she would have to turn the words into symbols, which is the very knowledge the gate
asks for, and the equations need a solver she would have to know how to drive. A child who can do that has outgrown
the program. Guessing: with the pause capped at 30 s and no answer more common than about 3 %, a child guessing the
commonest answers would need on the order of half an hour of steady wrong guesses; the gate is a speed bump for a
child, not a lock.

## Parent screen

{F5} (grown-up) on the map or the first screen (a tap on the label, or the Mac's corner menu
"Grown-ups...") shows **the grown-ups' gate** (below); its problem answered opens this screen. Tabs:

- **skills**: progress per Star Math domain (standards of grades 2-4 passed), the grade she is
  working at ("working at grade 5"), and a scrolling list of every standard with its mastery,
  passed marks, grade 5 in purple; a tap on a standard previews how it is asked (the question
  screen, for grown-ups only);
- **facts**: the fact grid for x or / and the number of fluent facts;
- **time**: minutes played (total and today), play days, problems, first-try accuracy,
  standards passed, and the optional **daily limit** (off, 10..60 minutes; when it is
  reached the game says "time for a break" after the current level or game), and the
  **home time** (off, 3, 6, 10, 15 minutes; 6 by default): how long she plays in her room and
  dress-up before a friend needs her outside (the home story). The
  calculator has no real-time clock, so a play day starts at the first visit after
  power-on;
- **setup**: the 11 and 12 facts, the theme pack, resetting the player (two taps), **where she starts**
  (grade 2-5, default 3; the placement in her first games finds her level from mid-grade 3), **school**: her
  grade (3, 4, 5 or not said) and the week of its year (- and +, 0..40), **twists**, **the voices** (cute
  babble, words, off) and **reading** (auto by grade, slower, normal, faster: "Reading time" below). With school said, a pacing map of grades 3-5 (`mq_engine.c` `pace_week`: the usual order
  of a year -- grade 3: multiplication and division, measures and place value, the facts 6-9 and tens, area,
  fractions from week 22, data, shapes; grade 4: place value to a million, units, multi-digit multiplication and
  division, angles, fractions from week 20, decimals, measures and plots; grade 5: decimals, multi-digit and
  decimal operations, adding fractions, multiplying and dividing them, volume, the coordinate plane) says what
  school has taught (`mq_school_taught`). The placement starts each strand at her grade (at most 4), lower while
  that strand's probes are not taught yet, never steps up into what school has not reached, and never asks a
  topic it has not taught (a strand with nothing taught is not probed: grade 3, week 10 places multiplication
  and place value, and leaves fractions for week 22). The engine's goals follow it too: what school has taught
  comes first (the facts are a thread all year), and a grade above hers only once her strand is there. **Tastes**:
  one order in nine, in turn, is a taste of a strand that needs little before it -- shapes (3.G, 4.G), time,
  data -- whatever the school week or the place she plays in (`mq_engine_taste`; the playtest's "shapes never
  appear"); `test_mq_coverage.c` plays two weeks at grade 3, week 10, in her favourite places and checks each
  comes in the first week at a light share. A page may preset it: `mq school G W default` gives every
  player who has not had it said, and every new one, that grade and week (a page's
  `SPARKLE_CONFIG.boot` or the calculator page's `console` preset); `mq school G W` sets the current player's (`test_mathquest`);
- **focus this week** (on the facts tab): what school sends home -- a tap on a row's number puts its whole
  table in focus (x 4, or / 4 with "show /"; the number keys 0-9 do it too, 0 is 10), a tap on a cell a single
  fact, "+ a skill" adds a skill (adding and taking away with regrouping, telling time, how long, coins,
  change, fractions, place value, area, perimeter, long products and sharing), "for this week" ends it after
  seven play days, "share" sets how many fact moments are focus facts (40, 50, 60 %). Focus cells are ringed.
  The engine gives the focus its share of the fact moments and orders (`mq_focus_share`), the focus facts'
  Leitner intervals are halved (fast mastery), the no-repeat rules and the history cache hold as always, and
  the child sees nothing of it: the games just happen to ask them. The tab shows "N facts in focus", how many
  are fast and how many known on two play days, and **"ready for the test!"** when all of them are (and the
  focus skills passed); then the share drops to maintenance (10 %) by itself. The play log lists every focus
  fact (seen, right, average time, fast, known on two days), the helper friend's moments per skill and the
  misses by misconception. `mq focus mul|div N | fact A B [div] | skill S | share P | week | clear` (tests;
  `test_focus`).

## Reading time

Every kid-facing text that goes away, or moves the game on, by itself stays up for its **reading time**
(`mq_read.c`, `mq_read_ms(q, text)`): a base (to find the text and start) plus a time per character, never under
1.5 s and never over 20 s. A tap skips, so a quick reader is never held up:

- a tap on a **toast** puts it away (the finger is the toast's from DOWN to TAP: never also a tap on what is under it);
- a tap (or ENTER) on the **cheer**, the why, the trick or the helper's step goes on;
- a tap on a **typewriter** line still typing (the helper friend's steps, a friend's story) shows it whole; the next
  tap goes on;
- "You already know this!" (a teaching moment) goes on at a tap;
- texts with a button ("next", "got it!", "yay!") stay as they were: only the button moves them on.

**Reading speed** (grown-ups, Setup tab; `mq_pace_t.read_speed`, saved; an older save's zero is Auto):
**auto** (the default: by her grade -- the school grade the grown-ups said, else the starting grade), **slower**,
**normal**, **faster**. The paces come from typical reading rates -- oral reading fluency (Hasbrouck & Tindal
2017, 50th percentile, words correct a minute) and silent reading (Taylor 1965) -- at about 6 characters a word,
set slower than either because she reads with a game going on around her:

| pace | Auto at grade | oral wcpm | silent wpm | here | chars/s | base | typewriter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slowest (manual: slower) | 2 and below | 50-100 | 80-115 | 125 ms a char | 8 | 2.0 s | 75 ms a letter |
| comfy (manual: normal) | 3 | 83-112 | 138 | 90 ms a char | 11 | 1.5 s | 55 ms |
| quick (manual: faster) | 4 and 5 | 94-146 | 158-173 | 75 ms a char | 13 | 1.2 s | 45 ms |
| quicker | 6 and up | 132-146 | 185 | 60 ms a char | 16.7 | 1.0 s | 38 ms |

The quickest (16.7 characters a second, 100 words a minute) is still slower than a sixth grader reads aloud. A
typewriter types about 1.6 times her reading pace (she reads along), and the line stays its whole reading time
from its first letter. Example: "Pip moved into town!" (20 characters) stays 3.3 s at grade 3 (it was 2.5 s).

What uses it (old -> new, grade 3): toasts 2.5 s -> by length; the cheer 1.5 s (Bubble Pop 1.0) -> the friend's
line (+ the combo's big words) at least the old beat; the oops line 4 s -> at least its reading time; the why 4.2 s,
the trick's steps 2.6 s, the helper's arrival 2.4 s and steps 6 s (typewriter 25 ms a letter) -> at least their
reading time; the game's demo and the arcade's and action games' first-time captions 3.2 s, the lightbulb 3.2 s, the
twist 1.6 s, the goal banner 3 s, "Whoa, slow down!" 3 s, the big words 1.1 s, the "yay!" flourishes 0.9 s, the
teaching moment's "You already know this!" 1.5 s and its beats 6 s, the ways' beats 2.6 s and done line 1.3 s, the
cashier's thank you 4 s, the kitchen's words 2.6 s and "Thank you!" 1.8 s, the toys' words 2.6 s, Bongo Beats'
demo 3.2 s, the photo message 4 s, the town's chatter 3.5 s, the story beat 3 s a line, the old quiz praise 1.2 s
-> each at least its reading time. A friend's story types at the pace (35 ms a letter before). The cheer neither
earns nor spends the fun budget (it used to spend it). Not changed (gameplay, not reading): the acted story before an
order (its words stay in the order's bubble), the action games' "Yes!/Oops!" and their questions, Bongo Beats' hit
words, the fun beats' length, the "+3" coin pops, the notebook's crossed-out digit.

Tests: `test_mq_read.c` (the table, Auto following a grade change, the manual settings overriding it, the setting
kept over a reload and an older save Auto, a toast / the cheer / a helper's step measured against the table, tap to
skip with DOWN and TAP both the toast's, a long session at every speed with no text too soon); the bot's
`read-time` invariant (`state mq_read`: texts that went by themselves too soon without a tap, all of them, the last
too-soon one's ms shown and needed). `mq read [auto|slower|normal|faster] [N]` (console), `mq settle` (tests: frames
until the cheer, the why, a trick or helper step or a way's model moves on by itself).

## Saving

Two profiles (name, buddy, hat, stars, every fact record, every skill record, stickers,
hats, reviews, time, limits: about 2.7 KB each) and two homes (coins, the items owned, the
outfit worn, five rooms of up to 30 placed items, set bonuses: 1 KB each, 128 bytes of it
reserved) and the learning engine's records (below) form `mq_save_t` (24.0 KB, version 6: the friends, the merge board and events appended at
version 5, the long memory and the stage's pacing at version 6; an older save loads as it is and the new blocks start fresh,
with 10 sparkles). The module copies it to the core with
`api->save_put` after every change (at most once a second) and at the end of every level and
game; the core keeps it in an arena buffer next to the module pool (`MODX_SAVE_MAX` = 96 KB since API
size 7, 16 KB before; API size 3 reports it with `save_max`, size 7 adds `save_size`, and the module
refuses to start when it is too small) and saves it with the session as the blob
`modx.save` (app/modx.c; version 2 has a u32 length, version 1's u16 length still loads), so progress
survives power-off, unbinding, module updates and even the module being removed.

The format only grows at the end. At bind: version 2 loads as is; **version 1** (the
profiles only) is migrated: each used profile gets the starter bedroom, 20 coins plus half its
stars, the old decor it had (one per 40 stars) and the stickers and decor of the old 12-slot
room placed in the bedroom; a **newer** version (a module downgrade) keeps the part this
version knows; anything else (unknown magic, too short) starts fresh profiles.

**The purse** took 17 of the home's reserved bytes (`mq_home_t` stays 1 KB; no new version): a save from
before it has `purse_ver` 0 and its coins plus a quarter of its stars become the balance once
(`mq_purse_migrate`; a version-1 save's stars were already paid as coins); an older module that played
the save since changed `coins` only, and the difference is taken over.

**The home story and the clothes rack** took 13 more of the home's reserved bytes (no new version; the save stays
24,556 bytes): `home_s`, `home_day`, `rack_day`, `home_min`, `errand`, `story_fl`, `errands`, `rack[3]`. In a save
from before them they are zero, which is the fresh state (the default home time, no errand, the rack filled on the
first look); her clothes, the outfit she wears and her rooms are untouched (`test_mq_homestory`'s `test_save`).

## Stories: little dramas for the cast

`mq_story.c`. Word problems are errands for the characters with a motive the child cares about:
a problem, a mild feeling, a fix she makes with a game action, and a happy ending. Fairness
("Momo baked 24 cupcakes for 4 friends. Dot got fewer and pouts! Make it fair: how many each?"),
getting ready in time ("Olli's train ride starts at 3:40 ..."), nobody left behind ("27 friends go
to Pip's party by bus. Each bus fits 6 ..."), the bunny hopping out through a short fence, a rug
that does not cover the floor, a recipe made more times for more guests, who made more cupcakes,
gifts and change, picnics, sleepovers, crafts, gardens. Feelings stay mild (a pout, a worry, a
hurry), nothing is scary or mean, and every story ends happily (a cheer, a hug, a dance).

- **Kinds** (`ST_*`, 23): groups, fair shares, how many groups, how many on one, times as many,
  buses (remainders, one more), full boxes, leftovers, take away after groups, share then more,
  add, take away, big products, division with leftovers, getting ready (elapsed time), how long,
  change, jugs, distances, fences (perimeter), rugs (area), recipes (fraction x whole), marbles.
  Five templates each; each kind has a fixed list of numbers its templates take.
- **Placeholders**: `{A}` `{B}` the two friends; `{fA}` / `{FA}` A's favourite food, one / several;
  `{kA}` colour; `{hA}` hobby; `{tA}` / `{TA}` favourite thing; `{pA}` her / his / their; `{oA}`
  her / him / them; `{n:word}` agreeing with the number before it; `{a}` a or an. Plurals:
  -ies, -es, irregulars (leaf, mouse, child, sheep, potato ...).
- **The friends**: A is the player's best friend (cast slot 0) or the world's guide most of the
  time, B anyone else; the names and traits come from the theme pack (built-in by default).
- **Fit**: the expanded story is word-wrapped into the three lines of the order board (font_small,
  300 pixels); if a long name makes it overflow, it is told with the first two friends. Tests
  expand every template for every friend, with 11-letter theme names and 15-letter traits.
- **Misconceptions as the story** (`mq_story_oops`): "5 each leaves 4 on the plate. Dot still
  pouts!", "Oh no! 3 friends have no seat yet!", "That's the inside, not the fence around!",
  "The big and little hands swapped! Olli would be late!"; otherwise the misconception's gentle
  name told by the friend.
- The friend appears on the order board during the order: pouting while it is not fixed (a frown
  cloud), jumping and dancing when it is, with B hugging (a heart).

| paw | 28 | Bongo Beats: a paw's tap on a bongo, the song's beat layer (param: 0 low, 1 high) |
| bass | 29 | Bongo Beats: the song's bass (param: the MIDI note, its eighths << 7) |
| chord | 30 | Bongo Beats: the song's chord pad (param: the root, its eighths << 7, minor << 14) |
| tamb | 31 | Bongo Beats: a tambourine accent (param 9: soft; cowbell and clap take 9 for soft too) |
| giggle | 32 | a squishy squished: a ticklish giggle in its kind's own voice (param: the kind, the squeeze 0..3 << 4, 1 << 6 a slow press building); at most one every 450 ms |
| sigh | 33 | a squishy let go: a happy "mmm" (param: the kind; 1 << 4 after a big squash: a wobble-boing) |

| music | id | intensity |
|---|---|---|
| title, map, cafe, arcade, boss, wind-down, play | 1-7 | 0-3 |
| rhythm | 8 | the tempo step: 90, 110, 130 beats a minute from the moment it is asked for |

**The beat clock**: Bongo Beats keeps a fixed tempo (`mq_beat_bpm`, `mq_beat_ms`) from the
moment it asks for the rhythm track; the web synth follows the same tempo, so no position has to
come back from the page. Hits are judged against the notes' times: within 60 ms perfect, within
130 ms good, else a miss (`mq_beat_judge`, unit-tested on a deterministic clock).

## Resolution independence

One codebase runs on the calculator (320 x 240, 1x art) and could run full-window on a Mac
(high-resolution art). Everything the module computes is in **logical pixels** of the 320 x 240
screen: layout, motion (x16 fixed point for things in flight), hit tests, particles,
squash-and-stretch, transitions. Only the drawing primitives of `mq_vis.c` (`mq_fill`, `mq_rect`,
`mq_line`, `mq_text`) turn them into physical pixels: x `scale` (the API's `display_scale`, size
5; 1 on the calculator), plus the shake offset of the moment. Text goes by **style** (`TS_SMALL`,
`TS_BODY`, `TS_TITLE`, `TS_BIG`; `mq_font`): a font handed in is drawn with the font of the same
role at the display's scale, and layout always measures the 1x font, so positions do not move.
**A sentence of runs** (the cashier's "That's " "12¢" "!", a colour and a drawn cent sign each; "+37¢")
is set by the platform's own widths where it has a font of its own: `MODX_HR_MEASURE` (app/modx.h)
gives a text's width in that font (the Mac page measures every printable character and the cent sign
of its four styles once, `web_sparkle_metrics`), `mq_hr_text_at` places each run's box side by side
from there (centred where the 1x line is), and an amount goes over as one run with a real "¢"
(`mq_cents_run`). Laid out by the 1x widths each run sat in a box of its own and gaps opened ("That's
  12¢ !"). `test_mq_render` (`test_counter_runs`) checks the runs touch.

**Sprites by id only**: the cast, the stickers, the home items are drawn by slot or item id; a
theme pack can carry **variants** of a sprite per scale (version 3), which a high-resolution
platform would draw instead of scaling. A unit test draws a scene at scale 1 and at scale 2
(nearest neighbour, the same art) and checks that every rectangle at 2 is the one at 1 doubled
and that the hit-test areas are the same logical rectangles.

Art sources: the built-in cast and items come from code (`tools/gen_mq_art.py`,
`tools/gen_mq_items.py`) as pixel grids; the games' props, Puff the drum sheep, the town, the
grumble and the toys are drawn from shapes (circles, polygons, rounded rectangles) and scale
smoothly. The 40 x 40 cast is rasterised again by `tools/gen_mq_art_hires.py` (every face, 320
pixels a picture) for the Mac page; the zoom (`mq_zoom`), the mirror (`mq_flip`) and the tint
(`mq_tint`) of the primitives reach the hi-res hooks too.

**Two sets of art.** The calculator draws the 1x pixel art; the Mac page draws a second,
resolution-free set: SVG in `modules/mathquest/art_vector` (`tools/gen_mq_vector.py`, `make
sparkle-vector`), one kawaii style throughout (soft gradients, round plum outlines around each
body, sparkly eyes, blush, glossy shines). The nine friends are drawn in vector on the cast's
40 x 40 design space, all nine faces each (the module's squash, shear, hop and mirror transforms
apply to them, so pout, dance and fly move as before); every home item frame is its shape calls
run again as vector (exact footprints), the window views and the bird cage redrawn by hand; the
stickers by hand. Pictures are found by the hash of the 1x picture (the page build maps each id to
its current pixel art, so a pixel redraw keeps its vector twin as long as the id stays). Things
the module draws from many shapes are named with `MODX_HR_SPRITE` (`mq_hr_sprite`: the box, a few
parameters; append-only): the town's buildings (closed ones greyed, lit windows at night, flower
boxes later), houses, lamps, fountains, trees, the funfair, the sky, hills and street, the worlds'
backgrounds, the games' bubbles, rings, karts, gates, pads, coins, the clock, the till, the oven,
the cake, the story's things and containers, wall and floor patterns, the squishies (their soft-body
points passed, drawn as a smooth curve) and buttons. A platform without art for an id draws the
shapes as before (the calculator never asks). Moving parts are rigs on the page
(`web/page/sparkle_rigs.js`) timed by the module's clock. `make sparkle-test` checks that every
built-in picture and every id the module names has vector art, renders at 1x, 2x and 3x, and keeps
the page under 10 MB.

**The Mac page keeps the 1x intent** (the render audit, `docs/audit/RENDER_AUDIT.md`, `make render-audit`):

- *Tint.* Dusk and night reach everything: `MODX_HR_IMAGE` carries the tint word (`a[12]`) and the page tints
  pictures and silhouettes with the module's own `mq_tint`; a rig is drawn as by day into an off-screen canvas and
  tinted afterwards (multiply, add, its own alpha again), except the town's rigs, which tint themselves (their lit
  windows and lamps stay bright).
- *One name, one thing.* Every sprite id names one thing with one parameter count (the Sky Castle is
  `town/castle`; the sky is `town/sky`); `test_mq_render` draws the town at every time of day and every game in
  every world and fails on an id drawn with two signatures.
- *Text.* `MODX_HR_TEXT`'s style word carries where the text sits in its 1x box (left, centred, right:
  `mq_text_c` and `mq_text_fit` say centred); the page measures the text in whatever rounded font the browser has
  (SF Pro Rounded in Safari) and fits its size to the box (down to half the cell before anything is squeezed);
  a single character keeps its shape.
- *Art in its footprint.* The clocks are rigs drawn to the module's radii (ticks at the rim, never at the
  numerals); the clouds, holes and coins match the 1x shapes; the lighthouse's beam sweeps.
- *The corner menu* never sits on the game: in a 4:3 window the play area is a little smaller, moved left or
  down, whichever keeps it bigger.

**Toasts, banners and pop-ups have lanes** (`mq_toast`, `mq_lane`): one toast at a time, queued (three wait,
the oldest goes first), shown for `TOAST_MS` from when she can first see it (a modal card does not hold it up),
in the lane the screen keeps -- the header's free middle between its title and the coins, the town's
next-unlock bar (the toast says what she won in its place), a fact game's ground strip, an action game's top
bar; a screen without a lane keeps it waiting. In a game the lane is the top line (`hud_say`): the twist's name,
the goal, a waiting toast, "Overtake!" and "+2 stars!" (in the game's name's place); the combo's big words go in
the order's band during the cheer, and the cheering friend hugs the one who asked there -- nothing new is drawn
over the field, the instruction, the answers or a page count. The demo's words (the arcade, the action games, the
bongos) take their screen's lane too, and the ghost hand points low on what it taps (its number stays in sight).

## The meta game: something new all the time

`mq_meta.c` -- what pulls her back for one more round, without manipulation (no ads, no
purchases, nothing lost by staying away, the grown-ups' daily limit respected).

- **The unlock ladder**: something new every few stars -- 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, 45, 55, 66, 78,
  91, 105, 120, then every 15 -- about every 1-3 minutes early on, every few minutes later: a
  present for her room, a friend moving into town, a coin bag, a new shop, squishy dough, a
  sticker (in turn). Never while an order waits; one banner at a time, with sparkles. The next
  one is always shown as a filling bar with its picture at the end (a present, a heart, a coin bag,
  a little shop, dough, a sticker) and "Soon: a new friend!" -- never a count of points -- on the map
  and at the end of a round (the near-miss pull).
- **Daily quests**: three small ones each play day from a pool of things to do (help 3, 5 or 10
  friends, pop 15 or 20 bubbles, catch 10 or 15 goodies, decorate a room, win a finale, play in the
  arcade, play with a toy, buy something nice), 10 coins each, all three a bonus and squishy dough. The notice board
  in town shows them as scrolls (a tap tells the quest). The worlds' goals are the chapter quests,
  with the finale's grumble cheering up and the celebration as the payoff.
- **The town** (`mq_town.c`): the map is a street about four screens long that she walks along
  (her buddy, big, with the pet that follows her everywhere): her house, the notice board, a
  building for every place (the Garden's greenhouse, the Post Office, the Bakery with its cafe
  table and tip jar, the Clock Tower, the Shop, the Art Studio, the Fair's tent, the beanstalk to
  the Sky Castle, the Star Harbor lighthouse) and the funfair corner (the arcade). Progress is the
  town changing (purely cosmetic: every building can be entered from the start): a place gets flower boxes and
  bunting at half its stars, a golden star, balloons and twinkling lights when its finale is won;
  the ladder's houses, lamps, fountains and trees fill the gaps; "The town grew!" when something
  is new since she last looked (`mq_home_t.town_seen`). Friends who moved in stroll on the far
  side and now and then say something about what she did ("What a Garden finale!", "6 in a row!
  Wow!"). Clouds and hills move slower than the street, lawn flowers faster (parallax). Tap a
  building: she walks there and goes in; drag or swipe to look along the street; LEFT / RIGHT walk
  place to place, ENTER / UP go in (`mq town X` for tests). A world shows its few games to pick,
  never a row of stops; the place grows fancier with her stars (see "Entering a world").
- **The pet**: every player gets a pet from the Garden set at the start (existing players on their
  next visit to town); it trots after her in town and rides along in Bubble Pop, Cloud Catch and
  Gate Dash, hopping when she cheers.
- **The cafe's tips**: a tip jar at the bakery's cafe table fills while she plays anything (a coin per 30-90 s of
  play time, faster as the town grows, at most 40: nothing to hoard, nothing lost); a tap
  collects it.
- **Arcade bests**: the fact games speed up with the catches and the streak and keep the fastest
  finish; the action games and every round's game keep their best ("New best!").

## The toy box: numbers as toys

`mq_toys.c`, from the arcade. Toys and puzzles where the maths IS the fun toy, to build a happy
feeling about numbers and patterns; no timers on thinking puzzles, no school words, clever
thinking celebrated in character ("Whoa, you figured out the trick!").

- **Bongo Beats** (reworked after the playtest: "it seems you just play with drums"; "it didn't
  explain how to play"; "I don't hear any cats"): **drum the answer**. A little band -- Miso, an
  original apricot tabby cat drummer (a round head, a cream muzzle, teal eyes, a striped tail;
  not any meme cat's design), and Puff, the fluffy drum sheep -- plays an original song while the
  orders ride in. Every bar brings an order: the cloud in the middle asks it, big, from the moment it
  is next ("7 x 6 = ?", or a skip-count run whose terms sit in their own slots, "21 28 ? 42", the
  unknown a highlighted "?" slot), while its "?" note scrolls in to the hit line. The four bongos carry four answers (the right one and the usual
  slips: a group more or less, a near fact, the sum); she hits the right bongo on the beat (the
  keys 1-4, a tap, a click). One hit an order counts:
  - right, on the beat: perfect or good, a mew pitched over the tune, the paw slaps, the answer
    lights on the note, a burst; three in a row is a combo (a burst and a happy trill);
  - a wrong bongo: a soft thud, the song waits and the answer shows after the beat; she drums it
    to go on (no points);
  - a note nobody hits waits at the line: the song (the lane, the tune) stands still -- it never
    ends without the maths;
  - hits away from a note fizzle ("fizz..."); mashing (four hits within a second, or fizzle after
    fizzle) pauses the song: the guide points at the order ("Listen! 7 x 6 is 42. Drum the 42!"),
    only its bongo stays lit, and quick hits are ignored until she drums it calmly. Points,
    combos, the song's end and its coins come only from right answers (a masher earns nothing).
  - The first time: the ghost hand points at the cloud, then goes down to the bongo with its answer
    and drums it, while the guide says "The cloud asks. Drum the answer!" (the song waits); later the
    one line "Drum the answer!"; "help?" shows it again.
  - After a right drum the whole fact is said on the stage for a beat ("7 x 6 = 42", the count "21,
    28, 35, 42"), the judgement small over it: she hears the song and sees the fact complete.
  - **A song of one table**: when she has a table to learn (the grown-ups' focus, else the newest
    table she has met and is not yet fluent in: the times-table runs, `mq_fact_run_table`), the
    song is that table's ("Song of 7s" in the line on top): its facts with the other factor
    shuffled (`mq_fact_run_next`), then counting by it from anywhere up to ten times; the end card
    says "You drummed the 7s!". A new player (the x0 / x1 rules not shown yet) gets a mix.
  - Eight orders a song; after it: "again!", **free jam** (the reward) or the toys. The facts
    drummed are recorded like any fact (a guided one as a miss).
  - On the calculator it is the same, seen: the tune's notes bounce in along the lane (higher
    for higher notes), the cat's mouth opens on each sung note and her paws follow the beat.
  - **What it teaches** (the user: "not sure what is being taught on the bongo drum game"): fact
    fluency -- quick recall of the times table, one table a song as schools practise it, and the
    skip-counting that the table grows from -- with the rhythm as the reason to answer quickly and
    the song waiting whenever she needs to think (never a race she can lose). A tap on a pad drums
    once (2026-10-23: a tap drummed twice -- a fizzle after every right hit; `test_mq_bongo`).
- **Bongo Beats' songs** (`mq_songs.c`): ten **original** pop-style songs written for Sparkle Town,
  stored as compact note data (a melody, chords, a bass rhythm and a one-bar beat per section;
  every bar 4/4, checked by the tests), 92-128 bpm, a verse (A) and a chorus with its hook (B):

  | song | style | bpm | form |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Sparkle Pop Party | dance-pop | 124 | ABAB |
  | Bubblegum Bounce | bubblegum | 116 | ABAB |
  | Lazy Cloud Lo-Fi | chill lo-fi | 92 | AABB |
  | Disco Whiskers | disco-funk | 118 | ABAB |
  | Starlight Jump | K-pop-style, high energy | 128 | ABAB |
  | Mango Mambo Meow | reggaeton-lite (the dembow beat) | 96 | ABAB |
  | Jingle Paws | cute holiday | 112 | ABAB |
  | Rainbow Anthem | the big finale anthem | 104 | AABB |
  | Robot Kitty Groove | electro | 120 | ABAB |
  | Picnic Shuffle | bouncy shuffle-pop | 100 | ABAB |

  **Originality**: every melody was written for this game; none quotes a real song. Each was
  checked against the well-known songs it could be confused with (children's songs, holiday
  songs, pop hooks): the openings that share a scale step with a famous tune (Picnic Shuffle's
  F#-E-D, "mi-re-do", then down to A, not back up as "Mary Had a Little Lamb" goes; Jingle Paws
  moves by leaps, unlike "Jingle Bells"' repeated notes) diverge at once in pitch and rhythm.
  Chord loops (I-V-vi-IV and the like) and the dembow beat are common building blocks, not
  anyone's song. No public-domain classics and no recordings are used: the Mac synthesises
  everything.
- **The band's sound** (the Mac, Web Audio; nothing on the calculator): the cats sing the melody,
  a meow a note -- a buzzy source through a formant that glides m -> e -> ow at the note's pitch,
  a light vibrato; a short "mew" for short notes, a held "meeeow" for long ones, a rolled "mrrp"
  for pickups (MODX_SFX_MEW). Under them the beat layer: paws on the bongos (MODX_SFX_PAW), soft
  cowbell, clap and tambourine accents, the bass and a quiet chord pad (MODX_SFX_BASS,
  MODX_SFX_CHORD, MODX_SFX_TAMB). The module plays the song's events along the beat clock (the
  sixteenths since the count-in), so every meow and paw lands on the grid of the hits (within a
  frame; the tests check it). The mix: the meows on top, the bongos punchy but soft, the pad low.
- **Jam**: free drumming with Puff and the pulsing beat; also a **fun beat** (`BEAT_JAM`).
- **Kaleidoscope**: draw with a finger, every dot mirrored round the circle; the number keys 3-8
  set how many ways (rotational and mirror symmetry). Between full frames only the new dots are
  drawn (dirty drawing: the flush is a few small rectangles).
- **Magic square**: fill a 3 x 3 square with 1-9 so every row, column and diagonal makes 15 (a
  turn or flip of the Lo Shu square, the middle 5 and a few more given); the sums show as she
  goes.
- **Cage puzzle** (KenKen-lite): a 3 x 3 grid, 1-3 in every row and column, coloured cages whose
  numbers add up to their sums.
- **Number magic**: Nova's tricks -- think of a number, double it, add 6, halve it, take away your
  number: always 3 (step by step with her number, so she sees why); the 9s on ten fingers.
- **Growing dots**: triangle, square and star numbers growing a row at a time.
- **Mandala**: colour the rings; each colour's share of the outer ring is shown as a fraction, and
  a goal ("Make 3/8 of the outside pink!") is celebrated.
- **Secret code**: a friend's message in numbers with a rule (letter + 3); the code wheel turns
  them back into letters ("YOU ROCK").

## The story acted out

`mq_stage.c` (`mq_stage_scene`), `mq_play.c`. When an order with a story arrives, it is **acted in
the field** before the game begins: the friend walks onto a little wooden stage with a rug, holds up
one of the story's things and tells it in a speech bubble (with their voice), the story's things
pop on one by one at twice the size (or as big as fits) with their number tags and the bouncing
"?", and a friend the story is for pouts on the other side. The order board shows the game that
comes next as a title. A tap or a key skips it; when it is over the game starts and the answer's
clock starts then (response times stay honest). **Its pace adapts** per story type (`mq_more_t.acted`,
saved): the full 1.9 s scene the first time she sees a kind of story (the bracelets, the bus, the
change), the same scene quicker (1.1 s) the next two times, then a 0.5 s flourish: the friend is
already on the stage, the bubble and the things pop in at once (`mq_scene_ms`, `mq_scene_el`). Props have little faces. Nothing countable is drawn
(the stage's rules hold; `test_scene` acts every stage kind). The zoom of the primitives (`mq_zoom`:
rectangles, lines, text by style, the hi-res shapes and pictures) draws any part of a scene bigger;
`mq scene off|on` for tests.

## The Sparkle Kitchen: fractions of a cake

`mq_merge.c`; a place at the start of the town's street (a candy cart with cakes on it; sparkles over it while
there is energy) and a button at the round's end when there are sparkles. `test_mq_kitchen.c`,
`tests/ui/49_mathquest_merge.txt`.

**Why it changed (twice).** First the user said: "The sparkle kitchen game is confusing. I don't understand what
is being learned or if it is just for fun." It was a merge board with three chains (a sugar cube to a royal cake, a
dough ball to a rainbow star, a seed to a rose arch) and friends' wishes for things a few merges up: merging was the
whole game and there was no maths in it. It became a fraction counter where two equal pieces merged into the next
size. After playing that, the user said: "sparkle kitchen is a bit weird. you can combine up to 2 pieces of pie...",
and "you can fill up the table and not have enough space to bake more." Merging kept a leftover merge-game rule: only
two EQUAL pieces went together ("Only two the same go together!"), so three thirds could not make a whole ("Two
thirds stay two pieces!") and a half could not go with a quarter. That is not how cake works, so it felt arbitrary.
And the oven baked its cake already cut, needing up to eight free places, so leftovers could stop it.

**What she sees now.** A friend at the counter with an order in a speech bubble -- "3/4 of a cake!", the fraction
written as at school (3 over a line over 4) beside a little cake of its kind -- an empty round plate, a "Give!"
button, the oven, a row of **knives** (each a button with a little cake cut in that many and its number: cut in 2,
3, 4, 6 ...), the counter (6 x 3 places), the words and the cafe's table ("to the cafe"). A walk through 3/4:

1. A tap on the oven (one sparkle) bakes **one whole cake** onto the counter.
2. She taps the knife "2" (it glows: "The knife cuts in 2. Tap a piece!") and taps the cake: it falls apart into
   two halves ("Cut in 2: two halves!"). She taps one half with the same knife: two quarters ("A half cut in 2: two
   quarters!"). Or she takes the knife "4" to the whole cake: four quarters.
3. She drags a half onto the plate, then a quarter: each **snaps in** after the last, round from 12 o'clock, so the
   plate shows three quarters of a cake's place filled and a quarter empty -- the picture of the amount, with no
   numbers and no total. A slice dragged (or tapped) off the plate goes back to the counter; the later ones close up.
4. "Give!": "Momo loves it!" -- and with the fewest pieces (a half and a quarter), "So neat! ... a bonus sparkle!".

**The model: cut and serve.**

- **Cut.** The knife cuts a piece into k equal slices, and k is what her rung allows: 2 and 4 at rung 0 (halves and
  quarters), 2, 3, 4 and 6 from rung 1 (thirds and sixths), 8 too at the top rung (eighths; before school's
  fractions unit the rung stops at 2, so no eighths knife). A cut is allowed when the slices are a size the rung
  knows: a half in 2 (quarters) or in 3 (sixths), a third in 2 (sixths), a quarter in 2 only with eighths. Otherwise
  "A quarter is too tiny to cut in 3!" or "A sixth is as small as it gets!" -- a physical limit, never a rule about
  what goes with what. This is **equivalence by cutting**: a half becomes two quarters in front of her.
- **Serve.** Any slices of any sizes and either cake go on the plate together (a half and a quarter; a third, a
  quarter and a sixth). The plate holds one whole cake ("The plate holds one cake!" past that -- orders are always
  less than a whole, so this never hides an answer).
- **No joining.** Pieces never join on the counter; dropping one on another swaps them. Joining was weighed: only
  "clean units" could rejoin (two quarters into a half, but two thirds into nothing), so a failed join would be the
  same arbitrary refusal the user complained about, and three thirds into a whole needs a three-piece gesture.
  Cutting plus serving any mix already teaches equivalence both ways (cut a half into quarters; a half and two
  quarters fill the same plate), so the plate is the one place pieces come together.
- **Cakes.** Pieces are `CK_CODE(cake, d)`: 1/d of a pink or a lemon cake, d in 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8. The oven bakes
  the pink cake for orders in halves, quarters and eighths and the lemon one for thirds and sixths (the look the
  bubble shows); the flavour never decides anything.

**The maths model, and why fractions of a cake.** Four models were weighed for the first rework:

| model | what the kitchen's play means | why not (or why) |
|---|---|---|
| values in the smallest unit (a cookie = 2 sugar cubes, a cupcake = 4 ...), orders for an amount | doubling | "worth" is an abstraction a child has to take on trust; orders at her multiplication level would need dozens of taps and sparkles an order |
| a baker's tray of rows x columns | nothing: arrays are not made by putting two of the same together | decoration again |
| recipe orders ("3 boxes of 4 cookies") | packing | she can deliver three boxes without ever working out 12 |
| **fraction pieces of a cake** | **equivalence: two quarters ARE a half** | chosen: true on the plate (no "worth" to believe), the equivalence 3rd grade asks for (3.NF.3: 2/4 = 1/2, 2/8 = 1/4, 2/2 = 1), an order is a real fraction to make (3.NF.1, 3.G.2: a/b as a pieces of size 1/b), small amounts (one cake an order, one sparkle) |

So the order is a problem of **S_PARTITION** ("make n/dn of a shape": halves and quarters; thirds and sixths; more
than one slice; eighths) at **her rung of its ladder**, made by the generator (`mq_generate_fresh`: the problem
histories and the no-repeat rules apply). The **plate is her answer**: the sizes of its slices add up to an amount,
right when it is n/dn -- any slices that make it (one half or two quarters for 2/4; a half and a quarter, three
quarters, or a third, a quarter and a sixth for 3/4). **The fewest pieces cut from one cake earn a bonus sparkle**
("So neat!"; `mq_merge_fewest(rung, v)` searches the cuts one cake allows at the rung: 2 for 3/4, 2 for 2/3, 3 for
5/6, 3 for 7/8; fewer, from two cakes, counts too) -- the equivalence is the shortcut, never required.

**Booked as an answer, once.** The first "Give!" of an order goes to the engine like an order in a round (as the
shop's counter does): the problem's history (`mq_seen_record`), the review (joins when first played; a due review
counts), the success window that holds her near 85 % (`mq_coach_answer`), the rung's own window
(`mq_rung_answer`: she climbs the S_PARTITION ladder by the kitchen too), the skill's mastery, the strand
controller, the **error patterns** -- three eighths for 3/4 is the bigger bottom number taken for the bigger piece
(`PT_BIG_DENOM`); the rest of the cake (1/4 for 3/4) is a slip -- and the grown-ups' recent orders ("Kitchen: make
3/4 of a cake", her "3/8", why "kitchen"). It is a made answer (no options to pick from); there is no typing. The
time counted is from the first slice on the plate (the baking and cutting before it are play), and the kitchen
never fast-tracks a rung. The fun budget is neither spent nor filled there (it is maths).

**A miss.** "Hmm... a little more, please!" or "Oops, that's too much!", the friend thinking, the plate as it was:
one more try. A second miss: the friend shows what the order looks like -- the cake cut in dn with n of them
coloured in the bubble, "Look: 3/4 is 3 of 4 pieces!" -- and she finishes it herself (two coins then, six on the
first try). Mashing "Give!" earns nothing: only a right plate pays, and only the first answer is booked.

**No give-aways.** Nothing on the screen adds up the plate in words or numbers: the slices are drawn as themselves,
snapped round a faint circle where a whole cake goes. A picked piece says its own size ("A quarter: 1/4 of a cake"),
never the plate's. The test draws the screen with six different plates for 3/4 (a half and an eighth, a half and a
quarter, three eighths, a whole cake, one quarter, a third, a sixth and a quarter) and checks that the words on it
are the same every time, that no "5/8" or a lone 5 or 8 is drawn, and that the only 3s and 4s are the order's own and
the knives' (cut in 2, 3, 4, 6). The knives' numbers say how many slices a cut makes, never an amount.

**The first time** (`mq_pace_t.demo_seen` bit `MQ_DEMO_KITCHEN`): Pip asks for 1/2 of a cake and the guide (her
face and words in the caption box, the ghost hand of `mq_hand`, the same 1.6 s gesture loop as every game's demo)
shows each step as its moment comes: "Pip wants 1/2 of a cake. Tap the oven!" (the demo's cake is free) -> "Pick
the knife that cuts in 2!" (the hand taps the knife) -> "Now tap the cake to cut it in 2!" -> "Drag a half onto
Pip's plate!" -> "Now tap Give!" (with "A little more: drag a quarter onto the plate!" if she cut it in 4, or "Too
much! Drag a piece back." if she wanders off). Each step's words stay while the step holds (no timer) and are spoken
once; a line about what just happened shows first for its reading time. That order is never booked as hers; after
it the demo never comes back. Later, nothing done for 9 s -- or "?" in the top line, or the "help?" soft key --
shows the goal ("Make 3/4 of a cake on the plate, then tap Give!") with the hand moving from the counter to the
plate, never at the pieces that answer it; with only whole cakes, "Pick a knife, then tap the cake to cut it!" at
the knives; with nothing, the oven. Otherwise the caption box shows what just happened (its reading time,
`mq_read_ms`), then "Tap a piece to cut it in 4!" while a knife is held, else the goal.

**The counter never blocks play.** The oven needs one place (the cake comes out whole); with the counter full a
spare piece goes to the cafe first ("Spare pieces went to the cafe!"), so baking never fails for room; a cut with
too little room does the same. Each piece sent is a cafe treat (the tip jar fills faster; a whole cake pays 5
coins). A new order keeps at most `KC_KEEP` (6) leftovers, the rest to the cafe -- room for a cake cut in eighths
beside them. Spares can always be dragged onto "to the cafe" too.

**Fair and calm.** The oven's sparkles come **only from playing** (a first-try right answer +1, a round +3, at most
60; a picnic day doubles them), never from a clock; with none she can still finish an order from leftovers, and the
oven says "Play a game to earn sparkles for the oven!". Nothing spoils, nothing has a timer, the friend waits as long
as she likes, cutting is free, leftovers wait for the next order.

**Where it goes.** Each order served: coins (6 the first time, 2 after a miss, 5 for the demo's), hearts with the
friend (+2) and a memory ("Thanks for the treat! Yum!"), a quest's "Help a friend". **Every third order a
present**: a real squishy for her shelf, then a plant for her room (the cheapest she does not have; coins when she
has them all). Spare pieces and whole cakes go to the **cafe** (drag them onto its table): they sit on the cafe's
table in town and make the **tip jar** fill faster; a whole cake pays 5 coins; when six sit there the oldest is
eaten. Today's **visitor** wishes for a whole cake (either kind, fresh from the oven and uncut) and brings a keepsake
from far away.

**Controls** (the widget library, `mq_widget.c`): the counter is an area the kitchen reads itself; the oven, each
knife, the plate, the friend, Give, the cafe, the visitor and "?" are widgets (every hit area at least `MQ_WG_HIT`;
a tap on a counter place is the counter's even where a knife's wide hit area reaches it). Tap a knife to hold it
(tap it again to put it down; anything else tapped puts it down too), then tap pieces to cut them; or drag a knife
from its button onto a piece. Drag a piece onto a free place (move) or another piece (swap), onto the plate or the
friend, the cafe or the visitor; drag a slice off the plate (or tap it) to take it back. Or tap a piece, then where
it goes. A real tap is a DOWN then a TAP: the DOWN only lifts a piece or a knife, the TAP acts, never both. Keys: the
arrows move round the counter and on to the plate (RIGHT off its edge), the oven (DOWN off the first two columns)
and the knives (DOWN off the others; UP from them back to the counter), the arrows then move between the widgets;
ENTER picks a piece and puts it, cuts with the knife held, or does what the focused thing does; 1 bakes; 0 sends
the picked piece to the cafe; CLEAR lets go of a picked piece or the knife (then goes back to town); F3 (help?) the
hint.

**Save.** `mq_extra_t` keeps its 384 bytes and its layout: the board's 30 cells (18 used now), `ck_who / ck_nd /
ck_rung / ck_flags` in the old wishes' six bytes, the plate's eight slices in the same bytes the merging counter
took from the reserved ones (eight eighths fill a plate). `mb_seen` says which kitchen wrote it, and
`mq_merge_migrate` brings it up once on load: 1, the old merge board -- its things go back as the sparkles that
made them (a thing of level L took 2^(L-1); at most 60), the cafe's treats become whole cakes, the wishes and the old
counts go; 2, the merging cake counter -- each piece becomes the same piece in the new codes (its pink levels 1..4
were an eighth .. a whole, its lemon 1..2 a sixth and a third), its fourth row moves up into free places, the plate
keeps up to a whole cake and the rest goes back to the counter, and what finds no place goes to the cafe, so
nothing she made is lost; both end at 3. A damaged cell, plate slice or cafe treat is cleared before it is drawn.
The order's problem is made again after a load from its numbers and rung (the same identity for its history).

**Console:** `mq merge open | gen | cut A K | knife K | move A B | put A | put CELL CODE | take I | give | cafe A |
visitor A | order N D [WHO] | energy N | demo on|off | state` (state `mq_kitchen`:
`who=0,order=3/4,plate=18,pieces=2,flags=1`; `order` sets the first rung that has it, and so the knives).

## Rounding: the method (Hop Along)

The user: "the hop to the closest round game needs more thought to actually teach rounding as opposed to just
picking the closer side", and "show the number, but not on the scale". Hop Along's rounding orders (S_ROUND10,
S_ROUNDBIG) now teach the method: name the two round numbers it sits between, find the middle, decide -- and the
middle rounds up.
- **The number is on its card, never on the line**: she cannot eyeball the nearer end; she works it out.
- **The help fades** (`mq_round_level`): (0) while the skill is new, the line shows both round numbers and the
  middle's mark and number ("the middle: 45"); (1) once half known (mastery 40, or 80 % right over 6), she first
  finds the two round numbers herself -- three pairs ("30 and 40", "40 and 50", "50 and 60", never below 0) with
  "Which two tens is it between?"; a wrong pair only wobbles with a hint ("47 is 4 tens and a bit more.") and
  nothing is judged; then the line and its middle appear and she hops; (2) known well (mastery 75), the numbers
  only: no line, the two round numbers on the landing pads. An error pattern of rounding being practised brings
  the most help back.
- **Feedback**: after a right hop the line shows the number's real spot and why it goes that way ("47 is past
  the middle: up to 50!", "42 is before the middle: down to 40!", "45 is on the middle: the middle goes up, to
  50!"). A miss is judged at once (two ends: a second guess would be no thinking); the friend asks the method's
  question, never the answer ("47 is between 40 and 50. Is it past the middle, 45?"), then the helper or the
  error-pattern moment.
- **Error patterns** (mq_diag.c, appended records `mq_more_t.diag2`, zero in older saves): "Rounds to the
  farther ten (or hundred)" and "Rounds a 5 (halfway) down", from the order's own number; the friend's moment
  shows it on its number line.
- No new teaching-moment idea: the ideas' records are fixed in the save layout; the scaffold, the hint and the
  error-pattern moment carry the teaching. `mq roundhelp L` forces a level (tests), state `mq_round`.
  `tests/unit/test_mq_round.c`, `tests/ui/49_mathquest_round.txt`, followers in `test_mq_diag.c`.

## Difficulty ladders

Every skill has a ladder of 2 to 8 named rungs (`mq_rung.c`, `mq rung S` lists them), each adding
exactly one complication to the rung below (adding and taking away: 1-digit with nothing to carry,
2-digit and 1-digit, 2-digit and 2-digit, a carry in the ones, two carries, 3-digit numbers, zeros in
the middle; fractions: pictures, then number lines, then the numbers alone). A rung's orders mix in the
easier levels of the dimensions it does not raise. She climbs when the rung is solid (five answers
there, the last four right, 90 %), quickly while early rungs are clean and quick, and steps back after
two misses in a row or under 80 %; the 85 % controller (`mq_coach.c`) and the strand controller still
choose which skill comes next. `tests/unit/test_mq_ladders.c` checks the table, that each rung's
orders really have its complication, and a simulated new player climbing gently.

## The notebook: column work like on paper

Multi-digit adding, taking away, multiplying and sharing played in a calm game (Treasure Heist's
treasure scroll, the till's receipt pad, the kitchen's recipe card) open the friend's notebook: the
numbers in columns, filled box by box, ones first, carries and trades in their little boxes. Every
step is told while it is new ("6 + 7 is 13: write the 3!"), then only prompted ("The tens: 4 + 5 + 1?"),
then hers alone with a "notebook" button; a wrong digit gets its column's own why straight away. The
help fades per kind of work (`mq_pace_t.ws_level`). `mq notebook [LEVEL]` opens it;
`tests/unit/test_mq_cols.c`, `tests/ui/49_mathquest_notebook.txt`.

## Teaching moments

The first order that needs a big idea (groups, sharing, leftovers, place value, carrying, trading,
fractions, area, decimals, angles) brings the friend with it in a few short parts (fractions: friends
sharing a round cake -- cut fair or not, one piece each of 4 is 1/4 -- then the bottom number, the top number, the same amount in different pieces, which is bigger).
Each part opens with a try: right, and "You already know this!" skips it; a miss or "show me" shows
it, the picture moving while the friend tells it aloud. "skip" jumps a part, "done" goes back to the
game. At most one moment in three orders; when the helper friend has come twice for the same idea it
is offered again; the lightbulb's "big idea" button replays it. The grown-ups' Time tab and the play
log (`big_idea,...`) show per idea what was shown, known and skipped. `mq teach [IDEA|pick N|skip|next|done]`;
`tests/unit/test_mq_teach.c`, `tests/ui/49_mathquest_teach.txt`.

## Widgets and gestures

Every game's controls are widgets (`mq_widget.c`): one look, one set of gestures. A tap presses or
picks (the nearest centre wins), a drag moves a draggable to its drop target, a wheel turns by its
arrows, a tap above or below, a drag or flick, or the Mac's scroll wheel; sliders, number lines and
clock hands follow the finger. Hit areas are at least 48 logical pixels each way, the arrows and ENTER
walk the focus, and the first time a game shows, its main widget is demonstrated by a ghost hand.
The Mac's scroll wheel and trackpad scroll act only on an adjustable widget under the pointer (a
lock's wheel, a slider, a number line, a clock hand, the rug's corner), the grown-ups' list and the
shop's pages; anywhere else a scroll does nothing (never arrows, a swipe, "back", the town's walker
or a game's steering). The page turns the browser's deltas into notches: one per mouse-wheel click,
a notch per 40 px of trackpad scroll (at most one per event), the trackpad's inertial tail ignored;
macOS natural scrolling is followed as set; Ctrl+wheel is a pinch; the page itself never scrolls.
The HUD line on top holds the goal, the demo's words and hints: nothing is drawn over the game's
answers. `mq widgets` lists a screen's widgets; `tests/unit/test_mq_widgets.c` plays every game by
touch, keys and mouse.


## Ways to work it: school's strategies as hands-on models

`mq_strat.c` (`mq strat ...`); so the game works sums the way her class does. Every Common Core strategy of grades
3-5 is a **way** (`STG_*`, 38 of them), told in the story as one friend's trick -- only the grown-ups' screen uses
the school names:

| Family | Ways (in the story) |
|---|---|
| adding, taking away | open number line (Pip's jumping trick), partial sums (Dot's sorting trick), expanded form (Dot's stretch-out trick), making ten or a hundred (Momo's make-a-ten trick), compensation 299 + 45 = 300 + 44 (Tango's round-and-fix trick), counting up to subtract (Olli's count-up trick), the standard algorithm at grade 4 (Dot's column trick: the notebook) |
| times | equal groups (Pip's bags trick), arrays (Pip's garden rows), the area model (Dabble's floor trick), partial products (Dabble's piece-by-piece trick), doubling and halving (Pip's double-half trick), known facts (Pip's head-start trick), the standard algorithm at grade 5 (Nova's column-times trick) |
| the rules of times | commutative (Pip's turn-around trick: both rectangles, one turned onto the other, "the same amount?", also "4 x 2 = 2 x ?"), associative (Pip's regroup trick: three gardens slid into one), distributive (Pip's break-apart trick: 7 x 6 split into 7 x 5 and 7 x 1 beside its sum), identity and zero (Pip's one-and-none trick), friendly numbers 25 x 4 x 7 (Tango's friendly-pair trick) |
| sharing | equal sharing (Momo's dealing trick), equal grouping (Pip's bagging trick), think multiplication (Pip's think-times trick), partial quotients (Dot's peel-off trick), area-model division (Dabble's floor-share trick); no long division before grade 6 |
| stories | tape diagrams for part-part-whole, comparison (more, and times as many at grade 4) and equal groups (Bramble's ribbon tricks) |
| slices | fraction strips (Momo's cake-strip trick), circles and rectangles (Momo's pie trick), the number line (Momo's hop-along trick), benchmarks 1/2 and 1 (Momo's halfway trick), unit fractions 3/4 = 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 (Momo's one-piece trick), equivalence (Momo's cut-again trick) |
| places | the place-value chart to hundredths (Dot's house of places), base-ten blocks (Dot's block trick), money as decimals (Tango's coin trick) |
| measures | elapsed time on a line (Olli's time-line trick), line plots (Bramble's X-marks trick) |

**The model.** Each way is a picture she works with her hands, built from the order's numbers: a plan of steps
(`mq_sstep_t`) -- a hop dragged onto the path (`GE_JUMP`), a thing dragged into its place (`GE_DRAG`: blocks into
houses, discs, digit cards, coins onto trays, a cube from jar to jar, a handful of groups peeled off the pile, a
number onto its part of the ribbon, a piece into the cake, an X above its tick, one number onto another), a tap
(`GE_TAP`: a bag filled, a round dealt, a piece frosted, a house's blocks slid together, ten rods glued into a
flat), a slider (`GE_SLIDE`: rows planted, the garden turned or cut, gardens slid together, a floor cut at the
tens, a cake cut into pieces or cut again) and a pick of the step's answer (`GE_PICK`). Every control is a shared
widget with the full gesture set (drag, tap-then-tap, the arrows and ENTER to carry and drop, the scroll wheel on
sliders, digit keys for picks); "show how" brings the ghost hand for the step. A wrong move is refused with its
own why ("A hop of +100 goes past the spot", "A flat goes in the hundreds house!", "25 and 7 don't make a round
number"); the first one counts the order as missed first time (as the notebook does). Drawn with the mq_vis
primitives, every piece inside an hr bracket with its own name (`model/...`, one look per name) whose vector twin
is in `tools/mqvec/models.py`; numbers are drawn after the bracket.

**The teaching segment.** The way's friend shows it on an example of their own: one or two opening lines in story
language, then every step done by the ghost hand while the friend says it (aloud on the Mac through `mq_speak`),
then "Your turn: try one!". "try one" skips straight to her own; a tap or "next" moves on. The turn-around trick
turns the rectangle smoothly a quarter round onto the other (a ghost where it started), with "2 rows of 4" / "4 rows
of 2" and 2 x 4 / 4 x 2 under each.

**When.** School's pacing map: each way has the grade and week of its year when classes usually meet it
(`mq_strat_grade`); with the grown-ups' school grade and week set, a way is open when school has reached it
(grade 3, week 10: the number line, making ten, equal groups, arrays, the turn-around and break-apart tricks,
sharing, the time line; not yet partial sums, fractions or the area model); without, her starting grade all
year. The standard algorithms only from their Common Core grade (adding and taking away in columns at grade 4,
multiplying at grade 5).

**Orders.** `mq_strat_order` (a new order): the first order of a way's skill once the way is open introduces it
(the teaching segment first, at most one introduction in four orders); afterwards now and then (every 5 orders
while she has used it under three times, then every 12) an order asks her to use it ("Pip's break-apart trick! Split
the garden to find 7 x 6 = ?"): `mq_prob_t.way`, played in the round's game; the model opens by itself and her
last step answers the order in the game (the bubble pops, the lock opens). The column ways open the notebook.

**The helper friend and the lightbulb.** `mq_strat_fit` ranks the ways that fit an order (a sum near a round
number: round-and-fix; a 1-digit add crossing a ten: make a ten; else the jumping trick, then partial sums;
close subtractions: count up; products: the floor, then piece by piece; sharing: peel off, then the floor; facts:
the head start or break apart, turn-around; and so on), keeping those school has reached. The helper friend shows
the best one with the similar order, step by step, and every other time a second way after it, then "Your turn!";
the lightbulb has a "Pip's trick" button that opens her own order's best way to work by hand.

**Lessons for the help paths.** `mq_lesson_for(q, skill, rung, order, ex)` picks the way that fits the order (else
the skill's earliest open way) with a similar example of other numbers; `mq_lesson_start` shows it as a teaching
segment ("my turn" skips it) and then she solves her own order (the help never gives the answer).

**Error patterns.** Two patterns for the diagnosis (`mq_diag.c`): a lost partial product and adding instead of
multiplying; where a way shows best where an answer went wrong (a lost piece of the floor, bags for times, the hops
on a path, the time line), the friend's picture is her order worked in that model (`mq_strat_diag_picture`).

**The grown-ups.** The Skills tab's "ways" button lists every way by its Common Core name and standard, the friend's
trick and when school meets it, seen (pink), used with the count (green) or at school now (yellow), six to a page;
the play log has a row per way met.
